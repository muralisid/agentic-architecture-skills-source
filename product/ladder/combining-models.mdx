## Give each model a clear job

A vision model may identify a candidate event. A retrieval model may find related evidence. A language model may prepare an explanation or choose the next investigation step. A forecasting model may contribute a prediction. Keep their outputs, uncertainty, and assumptions distinguishable.

## Preserve the evidence between steps

Pass source references, timestamps, units, and the model or configuration version that produced a result. Do not turn a detector score into a confirmed operational fact just because the next model repeats it fluently.

## Route by task requirements

Choose the smallest model or conventional method that meets the task’s requirements. Escalate a difficult case when a stronger model or a person can resolve a specific uncertainty. Include the extra delay, cost, and failure modes in the comparison.

## Inspect the complete chain

A strong component can still produce a poor overall system if it measures the wrong thing or loses context at a handoff. Evaluate the complete workflow using the original evidence and the accepted business outcome.

For example, [production measurement](/use-cases/production-measurement) needs calibrated measurements and reconciliation. [Safety coaching](/use-cases/safety-coaching) needs correct incident evidence and a responsible delivery process.
