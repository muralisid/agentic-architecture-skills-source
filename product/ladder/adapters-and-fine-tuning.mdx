<PlainTerms>
This is the first change that touches the model itself. You show it a few thousand examples of the task done correctly and its weights shift towards producing answers in that shape. It is the right tool for making behaviour consistent and for moving work to a smaller, cheaper model. It is the wrong tool for teaching the model facts, and the reason why sits in how the training actually works. It also has a side effect nobody advertises: models forget things they used to be good at.
</PlainTerms>

## What training on examples actually does

A model is a very large set of numbers, its weights, and generation is those weights transforming your input into a distribution over possible next <Term k="token">tokens</Term>. Supervised <Term k="fine-tuning">fine-tuning</Term> shows the model an input and the output you wanted, measures how far the model's distribution is from producing that output, and nudges every relevant weight slightly in the direction that closes the gap. Repeat over thousands of pairs and the model's default behaviour moves towards the pattern the pairs share.

Read that mechanism closely and the two big facts about fine-tuning fall out of it.

**It learns the pattern, not the content.** The gradient rewards producing outputs shaped like your examples. If your examples are good answers about your product catalogue, the shape includes fluent, confident catalogue answers. It does not include the catalogue, because a few thousand pairs cannot carry a million facts, and nothing in the process stores them. This is why a fine-tuned model answers questions about your business in a perfect house style and gets the details wrong: you taught it the style, and the confidence, without the substance.

**It moves weights that were doing other jobs.** The weights being nudged are the same ones that encode general capability. Push them hard enough towards your task and they drift away from things the model could previously do. That is catastrophic forgetting, and it is a property of the mechanism, not of sloppy execution.

## Full fine-tuning, LoRA, and QLoRA

**Full fine-tuning** updates all the weights. Maximum capacity to change behaviour, maximum compute, and the output is an entire model you must store and host.

**LoRA** starts from an observation: the change a fine-tune makes to a weight matrix is usually low-rank, meaning it can be well approximated by two much smaller matrices multiplied together. So instead of updating a large matrix, LoRA freezes it and trains the two small ones alongside, adding their product at inference. The trainable parameter count drops by orders of magnitude, and the artifact you produce is an adapter of megabytes rather than a model of gigabytes. Adapters can be swapped per task over one shared base, which is operationally the most attractive property: one hosted model, many behaviours.

**QLoRA** adds one more move: the frozen base is quantised to 4-bit precision during training, so the memory footprint drops far enough to adapt a large model on a single serious GPU. The adapter itself trains in normal precision. Quality is close to LoRA at a fraction of the hardware.

One belief needs correcting because it drives bad decisions: **adapters do not prevent forgetting.** Touching fewer weights sounds safer, and the published finding is that LoRA does not protect the model's general capability. If forgetting matters for your use, measure it, and see the repair on [distillation](/ladder/distillation).

## What you need before starting

**Examples in the low thousands, and their quality is the whole game.** The model will learn exactly what the pairs teach, including their errors, their biases and their shortcuts. A hundred meticulous examples beat ten thousand scraped ones.

**A locked <Term k="eval">eval</Term> suite that existed before training**, including tests for what should not change. Measuring only the target task is how forgetting ships to production unnoticed.

**A hosting plan.** An adapter needs a base model to run against; a full fine-tune needs serving infrastructure of its own. Either way you now own an artifact with a lifecycle: when the base model generation moves, your work needs re-doing and re-evaluating.

## The managed options narrowed in 2026

**As of August 2026.** OpenAI is withdrawing self-serve fine-tuning in phases, announced 7 May 2026 on its deprecations page. Organisations that had not previously fine-tuned lost access that day; organisations without recent fine-tuned inference lost it on 2 July 2026; remaining customers can create no new jobs after **6 January 2027**. Inference on existing fine-tuned models continues until their base models retire.

For most enterprises reading this, that route is already closed. What remains: managed fine-tuning through the major cloud platforms, and parameter-efficient methods on open-weight models you host or rent, which are unaffected. This is a volatile product fact and sits on the re-verification list.

## At the bank

The financial crime agent's narratives are correct by step 3, but they are correct the way a capable generalist writes: serviceable prose, inconsistent structure under pressure, and a tone that quality assurance keeps flagging. The prompt already carries three exemplar narratives, and at the margins the model still drifts.

**What the bank fine-tunes for.** Five thousand historical narratives, written by senior analysts and passed by quality assurance, paired with the alert context they were written from. The training objective is the house shape: how deviations are characterised, how certainty is hedged, how sources are cited inline, what order the sections run in. This is demonstrable behaviour, thousands of clean examples exist, and no paragraph of instructions has fully captured it. That is exactly the fine-tuning use case.

**What the bank refuses to fine-tune for.** Knowledge of customers, typologies or regulation. All of that continues to arrive through retrieval at run time, for two reasons. The mechanism above: the pairs would teach the phrasing of typology explanations, not the typologies. And a governance reason that is decisive in this vertical: facts baked into weights cannot be traced to a source, updated when guidance changes, or deleted when an erasure obligation arrives. Retrieved facts can. A regulated narrative must cite its evidence, and weights do not cite. Those facts live in [retrieval and governed memory](/ladder/context#memory-is-context-that-lasts) instead.

**The economics that make it worthwhile.** The real prize is moving the drafting task from a frontier model to a small fine-tuned one. At tens of thousands of alerts a month, a 7B model with an adapter, hosted on the bank's own infrastructure, drafts at a fraction of the per-alert cost, and the sovereignty posture improves as a side effect: alert data stops leaving the estate. One published head-to-head found a fine-tuned 7B model beating a prompt-engineered frontier model on a classification task, 88 percent against 31 percent, at roughly a fifteenth of the inference cost. Treat that as one data point rather than a law, and prove it on your own eval before you switch.

**What the bank measures afterwards.** The narrative eval, and a general-capability eval: summarisation of unrelated documents, instruction-following on unseen tasks. The second is the forgetting check. If it degrades, the repair is on the next page.

## The test for moving on

They do not turn the adapter into a knowledge store; current policies and customer facts still belong in governed context." />

**Adapters have closed the format and behaviour gap, and what remains wrong is judgement or knowledge.** If the shape is right and the reasoning is wrong on outcomes you can grade programmatically, go to [reinforcement fine-tuning](/ladder/reinforcement-fine-tuning). If the model still misreads your domain's language and documents, go to [continued pretraining](/ladder/continued-pretraining). If quality is right and cost or latency is the complaint, go to [distillation](/ladder/distillation).
