A model can explain why a pump might be vibrating and still be unable to help the maintenance team complete an inspection. The gap becomes clearer when we look at what the job actually requires.

## Start with a useful answer, then ask what is missing

Consider a proposed maintenance assistant. You ask, “What should we do about this pump?” The model gives a reasonable explanation of bearing wear. That demonstrates useful knowledge, but the team needs something more specific: which pump, what changed, what the current procedure allows, and whether an inspection was actually requested.

A natural response is to write a longer prompt, paste in more documents, or try a larger model. Each can help a particular problem. But none, by itself, supplies a missing asset record, permission to create a work order, or confirmation that the work-order system accepted it.

The Intelligence ladder gives you a way to work through that gap. Begin with the limitation you can observe. Change the part of the system responsible for it. Test whether the job now succeeds before adding more machinery.

## First, make the task clear

Suppose “What should we do?” produces a general maintenance essay. The team actually needs a short investigation note: observations, possible causes, missing evidence, and the next permitted check. Clear instructions and an example may be enough to produce that result.

**Why this matters:** you cannot judge the agent's performance if you have not said what a useful outcome looks like. [Instructions](/ladder/instructions) explains how to make that expectation explicit.

## Next, give it the evidence for this job

Now the note is well structured, but it refers to the wrong pump model. Repeating the instruction will not supply the correct asset record. The agent needs current, relevant evidence: the asset identity, recent work, readings, and applicable procedure.

**Why this matters:** general model knowledge cannot establish what happened to this particular pump yesterday. [Context](/ladder/context) explains how to supply the working information. [Knowledge and memory](/memory) explains how to make that information findable and trustworthy over time.

## Then, let it close a specific gap

The current vibration pattern might require another reading or a check of a work order. A static answer cannot fetch evidence it was never given. Tools let the agent inspect a record or request an allowed operation. A bounded investigation loop lets it decide which missing fact to seek next and inspect the result.

**Why this matters:** proposing an inspection and confirming its creation are different outcomes. [Tools and the loop](/ladder/tools-and-the-loop) explains this difference. Use a fixed workflow when the required sequence is already known.

## Adapt the model when a limitation remains

Perhaps the assistant has the right task, evidence, and tools but still cannot produce a consistent equipment-fault classification on held-out examples. That is a reason to investigate model choice or adaptation. It is not yet proof that training will help.

The methods below address different problems. They are not a sequence that every enterprise must complete.

## Stop when the useful job works

The path is meaningful because each change removes an identified obstacle. You may need only instructions and a database query. Another task may require visual analysis, a controlled investigation loop, and specialist models. Adding all of them is not the goal.

For the pump assistant, success means a supported investigation and a confirmed, permitted outcome with acceptable human effort. Compare that result with the existing process on the same kinds of cases. Count errors, review time, latency, and cost as well as successful answers.

The broader aim is to distill model intelligence into enterprise capability. **Model distillation** is one specific training technique within that work; it is not a prerequisite for becoming an agentic enterprise.
