## Murali Sid

**In short:** Founder and AI Systems Architect at fn7, writing this reference from production experience and from experiments anyone can rerun.

Murali Sid ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/in/muralisid), [GitHub](https://github.com/muralisid)) is the founder and AI Systems Architect of fn7, and the author of everything on this site. He works on how enterprises and software companies actually put AI agents into production: the architecture around the model, the controls that keep it inside its authority, and the evidence that shows whether any of it works.

The work here comes from two places. The first is building and running agent systems in production, including retrieval and context pipelines at scale. The second is a research programme run against public corpora, where the claims that came out of that experience were tested properly, with matched controls and published results, including the ones that did not survive.

## What this site is

**In short:** A vendor-neutral reference for two audiences, published as pages you can read and skills you can install.

Agentic Architecture Skills covers the architecture of the agentic enterprise: [seven planes across fourteen layers](/architecture), [twenty-five contested technology choices with verdicts](/decisions), and [nine context patterns](/patterns) with the measurements behind them. Every section is also published as an [installable Agent Skill](/skills), so the material can travel into your own agent instead of being retyped.

It is not a vendor site and not a consultancy brochure. Nothing is recommended on adoption momentum: a component earns a place only by beating the simplest credible alternative on evidence and economics.

## How it is researched

**In short:** Dated sources, labelled vendor claims, and published gaps.

- Every claim carries a dated source, primary sources preferred.
- Vendor-published numbers are labelled as vendor-published, because their methodology is usually absent.
- Product facts carry an as-of date and sit on a [re-verification list](/library/re-verification) that is worked on a schedule.
- Where the evidence runs out, the page says so. Six cross-cutting concerns have no complete published answer anywhere, and they are listed as open rather than papered over.
- Experiments fix their decision rules before any number is read, report every arm including the ones that beat the method, and publish the reversals. [How that is done](/patterns/how-to-test-a-context-design).
