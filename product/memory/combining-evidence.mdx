## Start with separate searches

Use the suitable retrieval method for each source. A work order may need an exact asset lookup, a procedure may need document search, and a vibration episode may need a time-series comparison. Bring their results together using verified identity and time.

## Do not compare unrelated coordinates

Equal vector length does not mean equal meaning. Vectors from independently trained models are not automatically in the same space. Use separate ranked results first. A shared representation requires an alignment or fusion method and evidence that it improves the task.

## Check agreement and contradiction

A recent inspection may contradict an older image interpretation. A sensor may have been replaced between two observations. Keep conflicting evidence visible and seek the observation that could resolve it.

## Control the combined view

The reader must be allowed to access every source exposed in the result. Derived summaries and cross-source representations require an explicit access and deletion policy. Do not assume that attaching a single permission label solves every combination.

## Evaluate the contribution of each source

Compare text alone, operational records alone, and the combined approach on representative questions. Measure whether an extra modality changes a decision correctly and what it costs to acquire and review it. Remove complexity that adds no useful information.
