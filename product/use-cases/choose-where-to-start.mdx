Start with a process that has a clear owner and a result people can judge. A visible business problem is more useful than a general ambition to “add agents”.

## Describe the work

Write down the request, the current steps, the information used, the decisions made, and the result. Include exceptions. Identify the part that requires adaptive investigation and the part that can stay a fixed workflow.

## Apply five checks

1. **Can we evaluate it?** Agree what a correct and useful result looks like, with realistic examples.
2. **Can we protect its actions?** Identify actions involving access, money, safety actuation, or formal regulatory records. Place the controls outside the model. Score the agent on [the risk profile](/architecture/risk-profile).
3. **Can we supply reliable evidence?** Check access, quality, freshness, and ownership of the information.
4. **Is the process inside one package or across several?** Check whether one packaged system holds all the data and permissions, or whether the work crosses several systems.
5. **Is a vendor agent generally available on your edition, or dated within two quarters?** Count only agents marked generally available in the vendor's release notes, and treat announced ones as unavailable.

An idea that fails a check goes into a preparation list. It may need clearer outcomes, better interfaces, or improved data before an agent is useful.

Checks four and five decide whether to wait for a vendor's agent or build your own; see [agents and packaged applications](/use-cases/packaged-applications).

These five checks are the three gates and the two packaged-application tests in [the use-case portfolio](/library/frameworks/use-case-portfolio), in the same order and with the same numbers. The six tests on [agents and packaged applications](/use-cases/packaged-applications) run in the same order too. They add "Does it need an agent?" as test one, so checks one and two are tests two and three there. Checks four and five keep their numbers there. Check three, reliable evidence, sits in that page's "Prepare first" outcome. Test six is the scoring that follows.

## Assess the organization

Use the [readiness assessment](/library/frameworks/readiness-assessments). Keep the six areas separate: a strong score in one area does not compensate for a missing control elsewhere. The complete assessment retains its 24 questions and deeper evidence-based mode.

## Choose a first portfolio

Use the [use-case portfolio](/library/frameworks/use-case-portfolio) to compare expected value, operating cost, risk, and supervision capacity. A collection of individually attractive cases can still exceed the team’s ability to review exceptions.

## Set the trial boundary

Choose a narrow scope, representative cases, a responsible owner, and explicit stop conditions. Compare against the current process. Keep a way to return to that process while the proposed system is evaluated.
