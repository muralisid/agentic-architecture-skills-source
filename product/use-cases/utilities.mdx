An unusual reading or an image alert is only the beginning of utility work. Someone must check which asset is involved, whether the observation is reliable, what work is already planned, and who should investigate. These proposed agents help close that gap between a signal and a supported work request.

## Electricity networks

Use dated imagery and an asset map to find inspection candidates. The agent checks location, previous work, evidence quality, and duplicates before preparing an inspection request. Field evidence confirms what the image cannot establish.

## Water and wastewater

Bring telemetry, operating history, and field observations together. The agent tests whether a signal is consistent with a sensor problem, planned work, or an issue needing investigation. Treatment and network control remain in approved operating processes.

## Customer service

A proposed assistant could explain an approved bill or outage update, gather details, and route the unresolved case. A coordinating extension could track the service request to a confirmed response. Keep account changes, compensation, and public communications behind their own approval rules. Start with the [customer-service blueprint](/library/blueprints/departments/customer-service).

## Assurance and resilience

Collect maintenance, inspection, training, and incident evidence with clear dates and ownership. Compare recurring findings across sites without treating similar text as proof of a shared cause.

## Connect the examples to established frameworks

APQC's [Utilities process framework](https://www.apqc.org/resource-library/resource-listing/apqc-process-classification-framework-pcf-utilities-pdf-version) provides an industry process structure. EPA's [Effective Utility Management guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-water-infrastructure/things-local-officials-should-know-about-effective-water) provides a wider view of water-utility performance. Our four areas are a simplified navigation aid, not the complete taxonomy of either reference. Reviewed 18 September 2026.

Select measures for the actual task: confirmed findings per inspection, duplicate work, investigation time, unresolved service requests, and total cost. Broader reliability and quality outcomes need evaluation over an appropriate period.
