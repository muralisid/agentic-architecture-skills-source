A GP sends a referral to a hospital clinic. It arrives by fax, secure message or e-referral form. The result should be a confirmed appointment, or a clear decline back to the GP. Today a referral clerk in the central referral hub carries the work. A clinician triages each referral. With an agent, the clerk's typing and chasing move to the agent. The clinician still triages, and the clerk still decides any doubt about who the patient is.

## How it runs today

| # | Step | Role | System | How the data moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive | Referral clerk in the central referral hub | Fax server, secure messaging inbox, e-referral worklist, shared email | A fax image, an HL7 version 2 message carrying a PDF letter, or form fields. The clerk opens each one on screen. |
| 2 | Find or create the patient | Referral clerk | PAS and patient index. Healthcare Identifiers Service (AU) or NHI (NZ). | Name, date of birth, and Medicare number or NHI keyed from the letter. A new record if there is no match. |
| 3 | Register the referral | Referral clerk | PAS referral module. Scanned medical record. | Date, referrer and specialty keyed in. The letter is scanned or attached. The waiting time clock starts under state rules. |
| 4 | Check completeness | Clerk for admin fields. Clinical nurse for clinical minimums. | PAS. Referral criteria such as Queensland's Clinical Prioritisation Criteria, Victoria's Statewide Referral Criteria, or HealthPathways. | A checklist on screen or paper. Missing items go in PAS comments. |
| 5 | Chase missing information | Referral clerk | Fax, phone, secure message, letter | A letter or fax to the GP. The referral goes on hold. The reply comes by fax and starts again at step 1. |
| 6 | Clinical triage | Consultant, registrar or clinical nurse consultant | Paper triage folder, PAS e-triage worklist, or the EMR | Reads the letter and attachments. In Australia, writes an urgency category of 1, 2 or 3. In New Zealand, accepts with a priority or declines with advice. Often done in weekly batches. |
| 7 | Enter the triage outcome | Referral clerk | PAS | Category and clinic keyed from the triage sheet. The patient joins the outpatient waiting list. |
| 8 | Acknowledge | Referral clerk | PAS letter templates, mail house, SMS gateway | Printed letters to the patient and the GP. SMS where set up. |
| 9 | Book and remind | Booking clerk, with automated SMS | PAS clinic templates, phone, SMS gateway | Picks patients by category and time waited. Phones the patient and books a slot. Arranges an interpreter and transport. An SMS reminder goes a few days before. |
| 10 | Report | Data and performance team | Data warehouse, state collections, dashboards | A nightly PAS extract. Monthly waiting list reports to the state health department or Health NZ. |

**Handoffs.** The clerk hands to the clinician for triage, then gets it back. The referral hub hands to the clinic. The hospital writes to the GP to ask for information, to acknowledge, or to decline. The clinic books interpreters and patient transport.

**Where reporting comes in.** PAS data reaches the data warehouse every night. The measures are time to triage, the number waiting, and the share seen within the recommended time. Queensland publishes specialist outpatient performance by category. Category 1 should be seen within 30 days and Category 2 within 90 days. Category 3 should be seen within 365 days (Queensland Health, accessed 15 October 2026). New Zealand reports Elective Services Patient Flow Indicators to Health NZ. One counts referrals processed within 10 working days. These rules were not checked in this research.

**Volumes and cycle time.**

- In May 2026, 362,886 people in Queensland were waiting for a first specialist appointment. That was 15 percent more than a year earlier (AMA Queensland, August 2026, citing Queensland Health).
- AMA Queensland calls the people waiting to get onto the list a "hidden waiting list".
- One ENT service at Logan gets about 700 referrals a month. Sorting them takes about 12 clinical hours (Metro South Health research abstract, undated).
- Waikato dermatologists handled more than 8,000 e-referrals in 2023 (Health Research Council, 2024).
- Receipt to triage takes a few working days, and longer when clinicians are busy (typical, unsourced).
- No current figure was found for the share of referrals that still arrive by fax.

**Pain points.**

- Fax and re-keying.
- The same referral arrives twice, by two channels.
- The wrong patient is matched, or a duplicate record is created.
- Incomplete referrals bounce back and forth.
- Triage backs up when consultants are in theatre or on leave.
- The GP and the patient do not know the status.
- Long waiters lose touch, and phone numbers go stale.
- Referral criteria differ by state and by clinic.

## How agents run it

One agent runs fixed steps. Model calls only read documents and draft messages. Fixed rules decide order, limits and status. The agent writes nothing to the PAS until a clinician has triaged.

| # | Step | Agent or person | Reads, and from where | Writes | Who approves, and where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take in the referral | Agent | The fax image through the document capture service. The HL7 message from the integration engine. The e-referral as a FHIR ServiceRequest or form data. | A new FHIR Task with status "received", linked to the original document. Each field keeps its page and position. | No one. Nothing goes to the PAS yet. |
| 2 | Propose the patient match and check for duplicates | Agent | The PAS or patient index through a FHIR Patient search. The IHI from the HI Service (AU) or the NHI FHIR interface (NZ). Open referrals for the same patient and specialty. | A proposed match on the task. A duplicate flag, with both documents side by side. | The clerk confirms any doubt in the referral worklist. The agent never creates or merges a patient record. |
| 3 | Check completeness | Fixed rules, run by the agent | Versioned referral criteria for the clinic, held as reference data. The extracted fields. | A list of missing items on the task. Status "awaiting information". | The clinic's clinical lead approves the rules before go-live. |
| 4 | Ask for missing items and match replies | Agent | Referrer details from the provider directory. Inbound secure messages and faxes, as events. | A secure message from an approved template, with a copy kept with the referral. The updated task. Step 3 runs again. | The clerk approves each message at first. The clerk sees any reply that does not answer the request. |
| 5 | Prepare the triage view | Agent | The referral. Recent results from the clinical data repository (FHIR Observation and DiagnosticReport). Imaging reports. Prior letters. My Health Record documents where policy allows. | A triage view. Each item shows its source and date. Gaps are listed. | No one. It is a draft for the clinician. |
| 6 | Clinical triage | Consultant or clinical nurse consultant | The triage view and the original documents | The urgency category or decision, in the PAS or EMR triage worklist | This is the clinical decision. The agent records it as given. |
| 7 | Put on the waiting list | Agent | The triage outcome | The waiting list entry in the PAS, through its interface or an HL7 message. Fixed checks run first. | The clerk approves at first. Later, a fixed rule does. |
| 8 | Offer appointments | Agent | Clinic slots from the PAS interface or FHIR Slot. Booking order rules. Contact and interpreter preferences. | An SMS or portal message offering slots. The patient chooses. | The booking order is a fixed rule. The clerk approves any booking out of order. |
| 9 | Book and read back | Agent | The patient's choice | A booking through the PAS interface or a FHIR Appointment. The agent reads back the booking number and status. | The clerk handles any failed or clashing write. |
| 10 | Report | Existing data team | The PAS as today. Agent traces. | Waiting list reports, unchanged. New measures: time per step, requests sent, clerk overrides. | The performance manager |

**What stays with a person, and why.**

- The urgency category, and accepting or declining the referral. That is a clinical decision.
- Any doubt about who the patient is. A wrong match exposes one patient's record to another.
- Booking out of category order. That is a fairness decision about a person.
- Declining a referral and telling the GP. It is a clinical decision and a legal record.
- Using My Health Record. Access rules and the patient's own controls apply.

At Logan, a model called ReferralAI suggests an urgency category to clinicians. A suggestion model like this is a separate tool. It needs its own testing and a TGA check. The agent shows such a suggestion only if the service has validated it. The agent still does not set urgency.

**Where the evidence is kept.** Every agent step is a <Term k="trace">trace</Term>, stored outside the agent and keyed to the task. Every write to the PAS shows in the PAS audit log, under the agent's own account. Every message stays in the secure messaging log. The original documents stay unchanged in the document store.

**What changes in the systems.**

- A referral staging worklist built on FHIR Task, in front of the PAS.
- New statuses: received, matched, awaiting information, ready for triage, triaged, offered, booked, closed.
- Event subscriptions for a new inbound document, a reply received and a slot released.
- An agent service account in the PAS. It can write only referral status, waiting list entries and bookings.
- HI Service calls use the organisation's existing NASH certificate and are logged per call. Check the HI Service rules if a vendor hosts the agent.

**What does not change.** The PAS stays the record of the waiting list. State waiting time rules and reporting definitions stay. The specialist team still triages. GP software and the secure messaging networks stay as they are.

## Where this sits

The [four questions](/architecture/agent-types) place this agent.

- **Role.** It coordinates. The domain is health referrals.
- **Permissions.** These are what its own credentials let it do. It reads referrals, identity details, results and clinic slots. It writes referral tasks, waiting list entries and bookings. It sends secure messages and SMS from templates. It cannot set urgency, decline a referral, or create or merge a patient.
- **Autonomy.** It is designed and permitted for delegated tasks (A2) on the [autonomy contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract). A clerk reviews its output, and fixed rules release routine steps. Urgency and eligibility drop to assisted (A1), where the clinician decides.
- **Impact.** It touches none of the four [deterministic zones](/architecture/deterministic-zones). The rule for decisions about people applies. Urgency, decline and booking out of order shape a decision about a person's health. A named person makes each one. A wrong patient match cannot be undone once the data is seen.

It is [Tier 3 (strict)](/architecture/risk-profile), because it reaches regulated health data. It reads untrusted input from outside faxes and messages. It reaches health data, and it sends messages out. That is all three parts of the <Term k="rule of two">Rule of Two</Term>. So it stays at A2, and it sends only approved templates. Letting templates go without review is a change, so the risk profile is scored again.

Its learning level is fixed (L0). It does not learn from cases. People change its rules and templates at the change stage of the [agent lifecycle](/architecture/agent-lifecycle).

Two of the [seven capabilities](/architecture/system-view) carry most of the control. Identity and policy gives the agent its own identity (ID2) on the [delegation chain](/architecture/delegation-chain). [Policy is enforced outside the model](/architecture/enforcement): the PAS account refuses any write beyond status, waiting list and bookings. Observability and audit trail keeps every step where the agent cannot touch it.

## Measure the result

Compare with the current hub on past referrals first, then in a live trial. Measure these:

- Time from receipt to ready for triage.
- Requests sent per referral, and replies that answered them.
- Wrong patient matches and duplicate bookings. The target is none.
- Clerk overrides.
- Cost per referral that reaches a booking or a clear decline. The [value and investment](/use-cases/value-and-investment) page sets out that cost.

Report patient and GP complaints separately. Do not read clinical benefit into booking speed.

## Start with a trial

Pick one clinic and one channel, such as the e-referral form. Run the agent on past referrals and compare with what the clerks did. Then run it live with a clerk approving every step. Register the agent with a named sponsor and owner before go-live. Move a step to a fixed rule only when its error rate is known.

## Evidence and limits

The steps come from known practice in Australian and New Zealand referral hubs. Step times are typical, unsourced. Volumes exist only as a state total and for single services.

Triage suggestion models are at validation stage. The ReferralAI agreement figure comes from a 2023 study. Vendor results are as the vendor reported them.

Two checks come before a build. Confirm the TGA position before showing any model-suggested category. Confirm the HI Service rules before a vendor hosts an agent that looks up identifiers.
