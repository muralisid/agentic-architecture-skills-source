## Begin with useful work

Select a bounded use case and demonstrate the outcome. Use the trial to discover the real information, interface, and supervision needs. Avoid building a general platform before those needs are understood.

## Share the capabilities that repeat

Identity, permission checks, tool access, evidence collection, task recovery, and evaluation may serve several use cases. Share them where their requirements match. Keep a specialist model or domain-specific knowledge collection separate where its task requires it.

## Change the operating practices

Assign a business owner for outcomes, a technical owner for service, data owners for evidence, and people responsible for exceptions. Define how incidents are reported, changes released, and work returned to a person. [Autonomy and learning](/library/architecture/maturity-model) are separate choices for each task.

## Account for purchased agents

Some work runs inside applications the enterprise buys. Check what permissions, logs, cost data, and controls those products actually expose. Do not assume all their behavior passes through the platform you build.

## Expand by demonstrated value

Move from a successful task to related tasks, then to a department or cross-department process. Recheck shared supervision capacity and cost. A successful pilot does not automatically prove that a larger or more consequential scope is ready.

## Keep improving

Use production evidence to identify whether to change information, instructions, tools, workflow, or models. Evaluate each change and keep rollback available. [Research](/research) explains current questions and findings that may change the design.
