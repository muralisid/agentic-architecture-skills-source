A patient is listed for a weekly multidisciplinary meeting (MDT), such as a lung cancer meeting. Or the patient is on tomorrow's clinic list. Before the meeting, someone must gather results, imaging and pathology reports, and outside records. Today an MDT coordinator and a registrar do this by copy and paste across many screens. The result should be one brief where every fact shows its source and date. With an agent, the gathering and drafting move to the agent. A clinician signs the brief. Specialists still read the images and slides, and the MDT still decides.

## How it runs today

| # | Step | Role | System | How the data moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | List the patients | MDT coordinator | Email, phone, EMR referral form, spreadsheet, MDT module | Referrals to the MDT come by email or form. The coordinator keeps a spreadsheet. The cut-off is a few days before (typical, unsourced). |
| 2 | Gather results | Coordinator and registrar | EMR, LIS, clinical data repository, scanned record | Screens, copy and paste, and printouts. |
| 3 | Get imaging ready | Radiologist, at the coordinator's request | RIS and PACS | The radiologist reviews and sets up key images. Outside images may need importing from a CD or another archive. |
| 4 | Get pathology ready | Pathologist | LIS. Glass slides. Digital pathology where present. | The pathologist reviews before the meeting. |
| 5 | Get outside records | Coordinator | Phone, fax, secure message, My Health Record, GP portal | Asks private labs, imaging providers and GPs for reports. Waits. |
| 6 | Write the case summary | Registrar or clinical nurse | Word template, or the MDT form in the EMR or oncology system | Copy and paste from several screens. There is no standard way to show source or date. |
| 7 | Meet | The MDT: surgeon, oncologists, radiologist, pathologist, nurse, allied health | Teams or video. PACS screen share. | Discussion. The coordinator or registrar types the outcome. |
| 8 | Record and send the outcome | The chair signs. The coordinator sends. | EMR or MDT module. Secure messaging. | An outcome note in the EMR. A letter to the GP. Registry data where required. |
| 9 | Report | Cancer services | Data warehouse. Registry. | MDT counts. Time from diagnosis to treatment against the Optimal Care Pathways. |

Clinic preparation is the same pattern, smaller. A clerk checks the results are back. A nurse prints them. A registrar reads them before the clinic.

**Handoffs.** The referring team hands to the MDT coordinator. The coordinator asks radiology, pathology and outside providers for what is needed. The MDT hands its outcome to the treating team and the GP.

**Where reporting comes in.** Cancer services count MDT activity. They track time to treatment against the Optimal Care Pathways. Some outcomes must be notified to the cancer registry.

**Volumes and cycle time.** An MDT may discuss 10 to 30 patients a session (typical, unsourced). Preparation takes several hours of coordinator and registrar time per meeting (typical, unsourced). No Australian or New Zealand figure was found for MDT preparation time.

**Pain points.**

- Outside reports are missing on the day.
- Data is stale, because the latest result came after the summary was written.
- Images are not available, so the case is deferred.
- Summaries show no source or date.
- The same patient comes back next week because something was missing.
- No record exists of what the MDT actually saw.

Since 1 July 2026, outside pathology and imaging reports go to My Health Record by default (Australian Digital Health Agency). Outside reports are now easier to find, where the service's policy allows their use.

## How agents run it

One agent runs fixed steps. It reads and drafts. It gives no treatment advice.

| # | Step | Agent or person | Reads, and from where | Writes | Who approves, and where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build the list | Agent proposes | MDT referrals as EMR orders (FHIR ServiceRequest). The MDT module. | A draft list, with the reason each patient is listed | The coordinator confirms in the MDT module. |
| 2 | Gather records | Agent | The FHIR interface for results, reports, documents, conditions and medicines. The clinical data repository. RIS reports. The PACS study list through DICOMweb. My Health Record documents where allowed. | A source list per patient. It holds each document's number, date, author and version. | Read access only for listed patients, on behalf of the named MDT lead. |
| 3 | Ask for missing outside reports | Agent drafts | The provider directory | A secure message to the outside lab or provider | The coordinator approves each message. |
| 4 | Draft the brief | Agent | The source list | A brief in a fixed template. Every fact links to its source and date. Gaps, conflicts and stale items are listed. No treatment advice. | No one at the draft stage |
| 5 | Refresh before the meeting | Agent | An event when a new result arrives for a listed patient | The updated brief, with the change marked | No one |
| 6 | Review and sign the brief | Registrar or presenting clinician | The brief and its sources | The edited brief, saved in the MDT module or as an EMR draft note | The clinician signs. An unsigned brief is never shown as final. |
| 7 | Review images and slides | Radiologist and pathologist | PACS and LIS | Their own review notes | Specialist judgement |
| 8 | Decide | The MDT | The brief, the images and the slides | A recommendation | The MDT decides. The chair signs the outcome. |
| 9 | Draft the outcome note and GP letter | Agent drafts | The coordinator's notes, or an approved meeting capture | A draft outcome note and GP letter | The chair signs. The coordinator sends. |
| 10 | Hand over follow-up | Agent | The signed outcome | Follow-up tasks, as on [care follow-up](/use-cases/care-follow-up) | The coordinator confirms. |

**What stays with a person, and why.**

- Reading images and slides. That is specialist clinical judgement.
- Staging and the treatment recommendation. That is a clinical decision.
- Signing the outcome. It is the legal record.
- Using outside records beyond the service's policy. That is a matter of consent and privacy.

**Where the evidence is kept.** The source list is saved with the brief. It shows exactly what the MDT saw. The EMR audit log records each read under the agent's identity, on behalf of the named clinician. Each version of the brief is kept. Agent <Term k="trace">traces</Term> sit outside the agent.

**What changes in the systems.**

- The MDT module gets "brief drafted" and "brief reviewed" statuses, and a place for the source list.
- An agent identity with read access limited to the patients on the list.
- An event for a new result on a listed patient, so the brief stays current.
- A written policy on using My Health Record documents for MDT preparation.

**What does not change.** PACS and the LIS stay the sources. The MDT makes the recommendation. The signed outcome stays the legal record. Registry reporting stays as it is.

## Where this sits

The [four questions](/architecture/agent-types) place this agent.

- **Role.** It creates: it writes a brief. The domain is health records.
- **Permissions.** These are what its own credentials let it do. It reads records for listed patients only, on behalf of the named MDT lead. It writes draft briefs, draft notes and follow-up tasks. It drafts outside requests that the coordinator approves. It has no emergency override access.
- **Autonomy.** It is designed and permitted for delegated tasks (A2) on the [autonomy contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract). Every brief is reviewed and signed by a clinician. Anything that looks like advice drops to assisted (A1), where clinicians decide.
- **Impact.** It touches none of the four [deterministic zones](/architecture/deterministic-zones). The rule for decisions about people applies to staging and treatment. The MDT makes them, and the chair signs. A wrong or stale fact in a signed brief could shape care, so every fact carries its source.

It is [Tier 3 (strict)](/architecture/risk-profile), because it reaches regulated health data. Outside reports are untrusted input. It reaches health data, and it sends requests to outside providers. That is all three parts of the <Term k="rule of two">Rule of Two</Term>. So it stays at A2, and the coordinator approves every outgoing message.

Its learning level is fixed (L0). It follows a fixed template and does not learn from cases. A recommendation feature would be a separate tool, with its own TGA check, added only through the [agent lifecycle](/architecture/agent-lifecycle).

It has its own identity (ID2). The [delegation chain](/architecture/delegation-chain) records the named MDT lead it acts for. [Policy is enforced outside the model](/architecture/enforcement): the EMR grants read access only for listed patients. Of the [seven capabilities](/architecture/system-view), [knowledge and memory](/memory) holds the source list, and observability and audit trail keeps every brief version.

## Measure the result

Compare briefs from past meetings with the summaries people wrote. Measure these:

- Missing items on the day, and cases deferred because of them.
- Stale items caught by the refresh step.
- Corrections the clinician makes before signing.
- Facts in the brief with no source. The target is none.
- Coordinator and registrar hours per meeting.

## Start with a trial

Pick one MDT, such as the lung cancer meeting. Draft briefs for past meetings and compare them with what was presented. Then run it live, with every brief signed before it is shown. Register the agent with a named sponsor and owner, and agree the My Health Record policy first.

## Evidence and limits

No Australian or New Zealand figure was found for MDT preparation time. The volumes here are typical, unsourced.

The closest named case, Oxford's TrustedMDT, is an approved pilot. Oxford says it needs more evaluation before clinical use. Microsoft's orchestrator is at research stage for tumour boards.

The design gives no treatment advice. A tool that suggests a diagnosis or treatment may need TGA listing. Check the TGA position before adding any such feature.
