A chat interface tells you how someone interacts with software. It does not tell you whether the software can change a record, contact another team, or complete a task. Start by describing the contribution you want.

<TeachingIllustration src="/images/use-cases/agent-roles-v1.webp" expandable title="Four ways agents help people work" alt="Four overlapping roles: converse, create, assist and coordinate. Permission is a separate decision." caption="These are teaching categories for this guide, not an industry-standard taxonomy or a sequence of maturity levels." />

## Converse: help someone understand

A patient asks about an appointment, or a utility customer asks about a service interruption. The agent retrieves the relevant record, explains it, and asks for missing details. The useful result is an accurate answer or a clear handover. A conversation does not grant permission to change the underlying record.

## Create: prepare something useful

An agent drafts a field briefing, a coaching video, or a plain-language explanation from approved material. Keep its sources and review state visible. Generated illustrations and videos are teaching material; they do not become evidence that an event happened.

## Assist: help within an existing workflow

A coordinator opens a referral and sees missing information highlighted. An engineer opens an asset case and sees relevant readings and past work. This corresponds to the earlier label **passive agent**, but “assist” makes the contribution clearer. A person remains responsible for the next action in these examples.

## Coordinate: follow work toward an outcome

An agent requests a missing record, checks the response, updates a permitted task, and follows up within an agreed limit. This corresponds to the earlier label **active agent**. Define which contacts and changes are allowed, when to stop, and who handles an exception. Coordination need not require multiple agents.

## Follow one example across the roles

A referral coordinator can converse to clarify a request, create a summary, assist a booking clerk, and coordinate missing paperwork. The [referral example](/use-cases/referral-coordination) brings these contributions together. The booking still needs verified identity, appropriate authority, and confirmation from the scheduling system.

Choose the [intelligence required](/ladder/choosing-an-approach) and [memory required](/memory/memory-types) after defining this work. If a checklist, fixed workflow, or retrieval search completes it reliably at lower total cost, use that as the starting point.
