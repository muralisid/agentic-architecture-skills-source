An electricity distributor must keep trees clear of its power lines. Aerial imagery or a ground patrol flags a span where vegetation is too close. A planner checks the asset, the open work and the image. Then the planner raises work for a field assessor and a cutting crew. The distributor owns this work, and a regulator audits it. An agent can carry a finding from the image to a proposed work request, with its evidence attached. A planner approves the work and the field confirms it. Nothing is cut or switched on an image alone.

## How it runs today

This is the cycle at an electricity distributor in Australia or New Zealand. It is typical practice, not a survey of one utility. Work starts from any of these triggers:

- The yearly inspection cycle. In Victoria, each responsible person prepares an electric line clearance management plan by 31 March each year.
- New aerial LiDAR or imagery.
- The run-up to the bushfire season.
- Reports from the public, councils or crews, and storm damage.

In Victoria, the clearance needed depends on the bushfire risk area, the voltage, the conductor and the span length. Energy Safe Victoria audits every year. It has been able to issue infringement notices since 27 June 2022. New Zealand has its own tree regulations.

1. **Plan the cycle.** The vegetation programme manager works from GIS, the asset system and a spreadsheet. The output is a list of spans by risk area. Contractor scopes go out in spreadsheets and email.
2. **Capture.** Contractors fly aerial LiDAR and imagery, or patrol on the ground. Point clouds and images arrive as large files. Patrol findings go in on a tablet.
3. **Measure clearance.** A vendor analyst or algorithm works on the vendor platform. The output is a list of spans with encroachment and priority. It comes through the portal, or as a CSV or shapefile export.
4. **Review at the desk.** A vegetation planner uses the vendor portal, GIS and the asset system. The planner checks the asset ID, duplicates and open work by looking across screens.
5. **Create the work.** The planner types work orders into the asset system, or uploads them in a batch. Each goes to a cutting contractor.
6. **Assess in the field.** An assessor, often a contractor, confirms the finding with photos on a mobile app. Notices go to owners or councils where required.
7. **Arrange an outage if needed.** A network planner and the control room use outage or distribution management and a switching programme. Retailers get notice by a B2B one-way notification in Australia, or an EIEP5A file in New Zealand. Customers get notice too.
8. **Cut.** The contractor crew cuts and records completion with photos on a mobile app.
9. **Close and pay.** The planner closes the work order in the asset system. A contract manager matches the invoice in the ERP.
10. **Audit and report.** Compliance works from the warehouse, BI and the records system. It prepares the clearance plan, bushfire reports, audit answers and cost reports to the Australian Energy Regulator (AER).

### Handoffs

The capture contractor hands images to the imagery vendor. The vendor hands a span list to the planner. The planner hands work to the cutting contractor. The planner hands outage requests to network operations. Network operations hands customer notices to retailers. Everyone hands records to compliance.

### Reporting

- The share of spans compliant before the fire season, from the warehouse.
- Regulator audit findings and infringement notices.
- Vegetation cost, reported to the AER as part of operating spend. This point was not checked against its source for this page.
- Most analytics sits in the vendor's portal, not the utility's warehouse (typical, unsourced).

### Cycle time and volumes

- Vector in New Zealand works with Tapestry. A roadside pole check fell from 30 to 45 minutes to 5 to 7 minutes. A Kawau Island survey fell from about 320 hours to about 12 hours. Tapestry reported this from Vector's data on 20 August 2025 [vendor].
- Powercor says it inspects its whole network every year. That is more than 70,000 km of line and about 570,000 spans (Powercor, undated, search excerpt).
- A Utility Magazine article gives 400,000 spans a year, with cutting at almost one fifth of them (undated, search excerpt). The two Powercor figures likely come from different years.
- AusNet reported 314,000 spans inspected in high bushfire risk areas over 12 months (local news, January 2023, search excerpt).
- Capture to cut takes weeks for non-urgent spans and days for urgent ones (typical, unsourced).

### Pain points

- The data is old by the time work is planned.
- Asset IDs differ between the vendor, GIS and the asset system.
- Duplicate work orders.
- Data is held by contractors and vendors, not by the utility.
- Work orders are typed in by hand.
- No closed loop proves the clearance was achieved after cutting.
- A missed span risks an infringement notice.

## How agents run it

The first agent plumbing in this area has shipped in asset and imagery tools, not in billing. IBM Maximo 9.2 exposes work order actions through a Maximo MCP server (12 August 2026) [vendor]. LiveEO's Treeline Agent can raise a work order inside its own app, in early access (2 July 2026) [vendor]. The design below uses that plumbing. The agent writes one thing: a notification marked "agent proposed".

1. **Notice new findings.** Agent. It reads new findings through the vendor platform's API or an MCP connector. LiveEO's Treeline MCP is one, in early access. It writes nothing.
2. **Match each finding to the asset.** Agent. It reads the GIS feature service and the asset system's location record. It writes any mismatch to the asset data queue. The asset data owner fixes the record in GIS or the asset system.
3. **Check existing work and context.** Agent. It reads open work orders and recent cuts from the asset system, through the Maximo MCP server or SAP APIs. It reads planned outages from outage management. It reads fire danger and weather forecasts. It writes an evidence pack.
4. **Rank and propose.** Agent. It reads the evidence pack, the clearance rules held as data, and past field outcomes. It writes a proposed inspection request with its evidence. The evidence shows the image date, the resolution and how clearance was measured.
5. **Create the request.** Agent. It writes a notification in the asset system with the status "agent proposed". This write is what makes it [Tier 2 (enhanced)](/architecture/risk-profile). The planner approves turning it into a work order, in the asset system.
6. **Confirm in the field.** Field assessor. The assessor records the field finding on the mobile app and decides.
7. **Arrange the outage and switching.** Network operations, in outage or distribution management. The operator approves the switching programme. The agent has no path here.
8. **Cut.** The crew records completion on the mobile app. A supervisor signs off.
9. **Check the close-out evidence.** Agent. It reads the work order completion, the photos and the next capture. It writes gap flags. The supervisor signs off.
10. **Draft the compliance evidence.** Agent. It reads the asset system and the clearance plan. It writes a draft report. A named officer signs it.

### What stays with a person, and why

- **Cutting on private land or a significant tree, and owner notices.** These are legal and community decisions.
- **Outages and switching.** This is the [safety actuation zone](/architecture/deterministic-zones#safety-actuation). People run it in approved control systems.
- **Site safety.** People are on the job.
- **Attesting compliance.** It is a [formal regulatory record](/architecture/deterministic-zones#formal-regulatory-records).
- **Paying contractors.** It is money.

### Where the evidence is kept

- Agent traces, in a store outside the agent.
- The asset system's own audit trail on notifications and work orders.
- The vendor platform's logs. LiveEO says its tools inherit each user's permissions and stay in the customer's tenant (2 July 2026) [vendor].
- GIS edit history for any asset correction.

### What changes in the systems

- A new notification type and status in the asset system.
- A service identity for the agent that can create notifications and nothing else. It sits in the [security and identity layer (R10)](/layers/r10-security-and-identity).
- An API or MCP connection to the imagery vendor.
- A queue for asset ID mismatches.
- A feedback loop from field findings to the ranking. The [learning flywheel](/architecture/learning-flywheel) describes this loop.

### What does not change

- The clearance rules in the Code of Practice.
- The clearance management plan.
- Contractor contracts.
- Outage and distribution management.
- Switching authority.

**The open gap.** No source names the asset or work system that these imagery tools feed at a named utility. Nothing found on 15 October 2026 closes the gap. Test the write path on its own before any live use.

## Where this sits

Answer the [four questions](/architecture/agent-types) for this agent. Its role is assist: it prepares work for a planner. Its permissions are what its own credentials let it do. It can read vendor findings, GIS, open work, planned outages and forecasts. It can write two things: a notification marked "agent proposed" and a mismatch note. It cannot create a work order, approve a cut, or reach outage and switching systems.

On the [autonomy contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract), it is designed and permitted for delegated tasks (A2). It does a set task and a person decides. Its learning is curated learning (L1). People choose the images and past field outcomes it learns from, and review each change before release.

Its impact is small. A notification can send an assessor to look. It touches none of the [deterministic zones](/architecture/deterministic-zones). Outages and switching stay in the safety actuation zone, with people.

On the risk profile, an agent that only flags is Tier 1 (basic). This version writes to the asset system, so it is Tier 2 (enhanced). Register it through the [agent lifecycle](/architecture/agent-lifecycle), with a sponsor in the vegetation programme.

## Measure the result

Measure confirmed findings per inspection, and misses found in a sampled check. Count duplicate work orders, time from capture to inspection, and total assessment cost. Compare with the current patrol and planning process on the same spans. Do not carry a vendor's reported benefit to a different network.

## Start with a trial

Take a historical set of findings with their field outcomes as the reference. Run the agent in <Term k="shadow mode">shadow mode</Term>, writing nothing to the asset system. Review its proposals with the planners. Then allow the notification write for one depot or region. Keep the planner's approval on every work order.

## Evidence and limits

The current process is written from industry knowledge, not a survey of one utility. The volume figures come from operators, vendors and search excerpts.

Most measured results in this area are inspection time per asset. Claims that outages fell come mostly from vendors. No independent evidence for them was found.

The write path is the open question. LiveEO and IBM have shipped the plumbing, by their own account. No named utility has said which asset system its imagery tool feeds. This guide recommends testing that write path on its own before any live use.
