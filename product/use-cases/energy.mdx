This page covers three areas of energy work: markets, planning, and generation and network assets. Each use case says who runs it and whether it advises or acts. It gives the measured result, who reported it, and whether it is in production or a pilot. It places the agent on this guide's two scales. It names the zone it touches and the control that must exist.

<Term k="autonomy level">Autonomy</Term> runs from A0, where a person does the work, to A5, where the agent runs alone under governance. <Term k="learning level">Learning</Term> runs from L0, fixed, to L3, learning all the time inside set limits. The [autonomy contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract) defines both. The [deterministic zones](/architecture/deterministic-zones) are the areas where a model may never approve. The [risk profile](/architecture/risk-profile) turns these facts into a tier. The four questions on [agent types](/architecture/agent-types) describe any agent.

## Markets

### Battery bidding

Battery owners in Australia's National Electricity Market use software that bids and rebids without a person signing each bid.

- **Who:** battery owners using vendor bidders such as Tesla's Autobidder and Fluence's Mosaic. Fluence names Akaysha Energy's batteries among its users (pv magazine Australia, 20 November 2024).
- **Advises or acts:** acts. The vendors say their bidders work inside limits the operator sets, with manual override [vendor].
- **Measured outcome:** none published.
- **Stage:** production.
- **Placement:** A4, L1 to L2. It acts without approval of each bid, inside limits people set. People choose what it learns from (L1), or its learning runs through a reviewed loop (L2). This guide's autonomy contract asks for L2 at A4.
- **Zone:** [movement of money](/architecture/deterministic-zones#movement-of-money) and [formal regulatory records](/architecture/deterministic-zones#formal-regulatory-records). A zone action makes it Tier 3 on the [risk profile](/architecture/risk-profile).
- **Control:** a <Term k="policy decision point">policy check</Term> and an immutable decision log before the market <Term k="gateway">gateway</Term>. The participant must be able to check and adjust bids on that path.

**What the regulator said.** The Australian Energy Regulator (AER) issued a compliance bulletin on automated bidding on 5 August 2026. It creates no new obligations. The participant owns every bid, however it is made. Bids sent straight to the market operator without a participant check carry higher compliance risk. Participants should record the logic behind each rebid. Third-party providers may be liable too. The update to the AER's rebidding guideline was delayed. So the AER published this guidance on its own. This page read the bulletin through a law firm's alert (K&L Gates, 10 August 2026). The bulletin itself could not be retrieved.

## Planning

Planning agents read documents, run studies and build evidence packs. All three cases below advise. Engineers or staff decide.

### Network models in hours

- **Who:** AgenticGrid, from Monash University with Transgrid and Essential Energy.
- **Advises or acts:** advises. The agents choose which studies to run. A conventional solver does the calculation. Engineers decide.
- **Measured outcome:** a network model of the Broken Hill area took about two hours, against about three weeks. Reported by Race for 2030, an energy research programme.
- **Stage:** trial.

### Interconnection screening

- **Who:** PJM, the largest US grid operator, with Tapestry, from Alphabet. The partnership was announced in April 2025.
- **Advises or acts:** advises. Each finding links to its source pages. PJM staff make the compliance call.
- **Measured outcome:** 811 generation filings, about 220 gigawatts, screened for site control in under an hour. That is Tapestry's own tally [vendor].
- **Stage:** production for this screen, by the vendor's account.

### Interconnection study data

- **Who:** Duke Energy with AWS.
- **Advises or acts:** advises. Agents run parts of the study workflow on Duke's own simulation software and grid models. Duke says its engineers make all final engineering decisions.
- **Measured outcome:** data preparation cut from about two weeks to hours, by AWS's account (T&D World, 18 September 2026) [vendor].
- **Stage:** not stated in the report.

**All three:** A2, L1 to L2. The agent does a set task and a person decides. The network studies touch no zone. The PJM screen feeds a formal queue decision in the [formal records zone](/architecture/deterministic-zones#formal-regulatory-records). PJM staff make that decision. The control: conventional solvers do the calculation, every finding links to its source, and engineers decide.

## Generation and network assets

### Self-healing grid: the rule-based baseline

- **Who:** Duke Energy, announced 5 February 2026.
- **Advises or acts:** acts alone. It isolates faults and restores supply.
- **Measured outcome:** about 2.2 million customer outages avoided and 5.2 million outage hours saved in 2025. That is Duke's own count.
- **Stage:** production.
- **Placement:** not an agent. The announcement does not say AI. Self-healing usually means fixed switching logic. It is the rule-based autonomy regulators already accept.
- **Zone:** [safety actuation](/architecture/deterministic-zones#safety-actuation).
- **Control:** none new. The existing protection and switching rules apply.

### Outage request review

- **Who:** CAISO, the California grid operator, with review agents built by OATI.
- **Advises or acts:** advises. Operators keep final authority.
- **Measured outcome:** more than 500 next-day outage requests analysed in under eight minutes a night, by OATI's account [vendor]. In July 2025 CAISO reported no results yet and called itself cautiously optimistic (Latitude Media, 28 July 2025).
- **Stage:** production, as stated by the vendor.
- **Placement:** A2, L1. It does a set task, and people choose what it learns from.
- **Zone:** none.
- **Control:** each agent's output is checked on its own, and the operator decides.

### Where the line holds

Schneider Electric announced its Grid AI Assistant on 4 June 2026. It says every operational action stays subject to user review [vendor]. It names no utility. The research behind this page found no utility letting an AI agent operate protection or switching. The main guidance on AI and functional safety is ISO/IEC TR 5469. It is a technical report and is not binding. This guide's position: agents stay on the information path, and people and certified systems keep control.

## Separate potential value from proof

Measure accepted findings, false alerts, investigation time, review cost, and useful work packages. Measure avoided loss only against a defensible comparison. Most figures on this page come from the vendor or the operator. None is independently audited. The research behind this page found no independent figure showing AI cut outage time at a named utility.

Adoption is early. A PwC study reported in April 2026 found 61 percent of utilities still in test or pilot. None called themselves advanced. The study was read through a consultancy summary (innobu, 26 September 2026).

The IEA's [Energy and AI report, 2025](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/ai-for-energy-optimisation-and-innovation) surveys AI across energy. It notes how hard it is to generalize benefits beyond single cases. It does not validate the agent workflows proposed here. Reviewed 18 September 2026.
