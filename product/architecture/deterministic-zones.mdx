## The principle

**Models may inform, never decide.** In four zones the decision is made by deterministic rules over verifiable credentials and policies, and model output enters only as an advisory signal. This is not caution ahead of the technology; three of the four zones already run machine-learning signals inside them today, with the decision rule staying deterministic. The boundary sits on the decision, not on the presence of a model.

<Callout title="Why the boundary must be architectural">
Command and data travel through a language model as the same kind of token, so the model cannot be trained into a reliable separation between instructions and content (Bhattarai and Vu, Feb 2026). Authorization that depends on model behaviour is therefore an exploit-discovery problem, not a control. The zones move the decision outside the model, where it can be evaluated, versioned, and audited like any other rule.
</Callout>

Each zone below follows the same shape: what sits inside it, where the line runs, the steps that implement it, and the actions that stay off the table at any autonomy level.

## Access and entitlements

What sits inside: authorization decisions, entitlement grants and changes, role assignments, conditional-access outcomes, and approval of just-in-time elevation. Identity platforms already feed machine-learned risk scores into this zone; the score informs, the deterministic conditional-access policy decides.

| The line | In this zone |
|---|---|
| <I name="check" />&nbsp;**Decides** | A policy decision point evaluating policy-as-code over verified identity, in the tool-call path, failing closed |
| <I name="activity" />&nbsp;**Advises** | Risk scores, anomaly signals, and any model output, entering as inputs to the rule |
| <I name="bot" />&nbsp;**The agent's job** | Request the action with its own identity and context; accept the decision as terminal |

### How to implement it

<Steps>
<Step>
**Enumerate the consequential actions.** For every agent, list the tool calls that grant, change, or exercise access. This list is the zone's surface; anything on it routes through the decision point.
</Step>
<Step>
**Put the decision in the path, not in the prompt.** The policy decision point sits behind [the gateway](/architecture/enforcement#tier-1-the-gateway), so no tool is reachable without a decision. Instructions to the model are advice; the gateway is enforcement.
</Step>
<Step>
**Express policy as code.** Cedar-class engines give sub-millisecond, formally verified evaluation; OPA handles richer joins. Policies are versioned and reviewed like any production change ([the policy decision point](/architecture/enforcement#tier-2-the-policy-decision-point)).
</Step>
<Step>
**Fail closed and log every decision.** A policy-engine outage stops consequential actions rather than waving them through, and each decision lands in the audit record that [the evidence posture](/layers/r11-governance-risk-sovereignty#the-two-tier-evidence-posture) requires.
</Step>
<Step>
**Test the bypass.** Red-team the path with the specific goal of reaching a tool without a policy decision. The identity mechanics behind this step live on [the identity chain](/architecture/identity-chain).
</Step>
</Steps>

### What the agent may never do

- <I name="x" /> Grant, extend, or approve its own access, including its own elevation requests.
- <I name="x" /> Mint or modify entitlements for any identity, human or agent.
- <I name="x" /> Treat a deny as an obstacle. A deny is terminal; the appeal path is a human.

## Movement of money

What sits inside: payment execution, transfers, refunds, and procurement commitments. The convergent proof arrived in 2025 and 2026, when three networks built agentic payment stacks independently and landed on the same shape: issuer-anchored delegation tokens scoped by amount, merchant, and category (Visa Trusted Agent Protocol, Oct 2025); agentic tokens bound to agent, merchant, and consent, revocable in real time (Mastercard Agent Pay, Apr 2025); cryptographically signed W3C Verifiable Credential mandates carrying hard constraints (Google AP2, Sep 2025, 60+ partners). All three are vendor artifacts, and all three are deterministic authorization consuming probabilistic signals: card networks already score every transaction with machine learning, and authorization stays rule-bound.

| The line | In this zone |
|---|---|
| <I name="check" />&nbsp;**Decides** | The rail or issuer, validating a scoped mandate: amount cap, merchant or category scope, expiry, revocability |
| <I name="activity" />&nbsp;**Advises** | Fraud and risk models scoring the transaction inside the network |
| <I name="bot" />&nbsp;**The agent's job** | Assemble the purchase, present the mandate, stop at its edges |

### How to implement it

<Steps>
<Step>
**Remove raw credentials from the agent.** An agent never holds card numbers or banking credentials. Each agent gets a scoped payment instrument tied to its identity, so revocation is one act, not a card reissue.
</Step>
<Step>
**Make the mandate an artifact.** Amount cap, merchant or category scope, expiry, and the revocation path, written down, signed, and stored before first use. The protocol details are on [the mandate-bound payment stack](/layers/r10-security-and-identity#the-mandate-bound-payment-stack).
</Step>
<Step>
**Validate outside the agent.** The issuer or rail checks the mandate deterministically. If your rail cannot, the deterministic check moves to your own payment service in front of it; the agent-side prompt is never the control.
</Step>
<Step>
**Confirm intent upstream.** New payees and above-threshold amounts get a human confirmation before the mandate is exercised, at the autonomy level [the contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract) assigns. The caveat below is the reason this step survives perfect gates.
</Step>
<Step>
**Retain mandate logs as evidence.** Issuance, exercise, and revocation events are compliance records with the same retention discipline as any payment audit trail. The customer-facing flow is covered under [agentic commerce](/layers/r09-experience-and-channels#agentic-commerce).
</Step>
</Steps>

### What the agent may never do

- <I name="x" /> Exceed, rewrite, or re-scope a mandate, or issue one to itself.
- <I name="x" /> Split a payment to stay under a cap; velocity limits are part of the mandate.
- <I name="x" /> Pay a payee introduced by untrusted content. Payee changes are a human decision, because injection is the attack that works inside mandate bounds.

## Safety actuation

What sits inside: safety instrumented systems, interlocks, protection layers, and any actuation whose failure harms people or plant. This is the only zone where the standards exclude machine learning from the function itself: IEC 61511 for safety instrumented systems, ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024 for AI in functional safety. Joint guidance from six national cyber agencies (Dec 2025) says it plainly: AI should augment, not autonomously control, safety-critical actions, with the ability to revert to manual or deterministic control preserved at all times.

The honest counterexample is bounded and instructive: one vendor's reinforcement-learning controller holds direct closed-loop control at two named plants. It earned that with a small action space on a well-understood unit, training against a high-fidelity simulator, formal evaluation before commissioning, and the independent safety instrumented system untouched underneath it. It is not a language model, and it changes nothing about this zone: control authority stays with humans and deterministic systems for LLM-based agents.

| The line | In this zone |
|---|---|
| <I name="check" />&nbsp;**Decides** | Operators, interlocks, and the safety instrumented system, exactly as the safety case certifies |
| <I name="activity" />&nbsp;**Advises** | Agent analysis arriving through a validated presentation layer on the information path |
| <I name="bot" />&nbsp;**The agent's job** | Read sanitized outbound data, propose and explain, with full attribution of every proposal |

### How to implement it

<Steps>
<Step>
**Map the protection layers.** SIS, interlocks, and permit-to-work systems are declared out of agent reach in the architecture, enforced at the network: unidirectional or push-based paths that preserve OT segmentation ([the OT boundary](/layers/r05-lob-and-ot#the-ot-boundary-four-principles)).
</Step>
<Step>
**Keep the agent on the information path.** Sanitized OT data flows outbound to separate secured systems where the agent works; nothing flows back to actuation. Read paths are brokered, never direct ([CD-18](/decisions#cd-18)).
</Step>
<Step>
**Validate before display.** Agent output reaches operators through the alert channel only after a rule-based or digital-twin validation stage ([the R05 validation loop](/layers/r05-lob-and-ot#the-validation-loop)), with attribution of who or what proposed each action.
</Step>
<Step>
**Keep the safety case clean.** Agent output is never credited as an independent protection layer. The layer-of-protection accounting that certified the plant does not change because an agent now reads the historian.
</Step>
<Step>
**Retain the disable path.** The agent layer can be switched off without touching plant operation, and the reversion drill is rehearsed like any other emergency procedure.
</Step>
</Steps>

### What the agent may never do

- <I name="x" /> Write to actuators, controllers, or the safety instrumented system, at any autonomy level.
- <I name="x" /> Acknowledge, shelve, or suppress alarms on an operator's behalf.
- <I name="x" /> Appear in the safety case as a protection layer or as justification for removing one.

## Formal regulatory records

What sits inside: regulatory filings, disclosures, books and records, safety cases, and any document a regulator or third party relies on. The subtlety of this zone: records regimes (FINRA Notice 24-09, ESMA's 2024 statement) mandate accountability and immutable records, not deterministic generation. Drafting with a model is permitted; attestation is a named human act. The cautionary tale is public: AI-generated content with fabricated citations reaching a government deliverable cost a professional-services firm a partial refund and its reputation for care.

| The line | In this zone |
|---|---|
| <I name="check" />&nbsp;**Decides** | A named, accountable human attesting; an authorized system of record holding the immutable artifact |
| <I name="activity" />&nbsp;**Advises** | Model drafts, summaries, and checks, each carrying provenance back to sources |
| <I name="bot" />&nbsp;**The agent's job** | Draft with citations that resolve, assemble evidence, flag gaps, and stop before the signature |

### How to implement it

<Steps>
<Step>
**Inventory the record-producing processes.** Filings, disclosures, regulated communications, and safety documentation, each mapped to the regime that governs it and the system of record that holds it.
</Step>
<Step>
**Split drafting from attestation in the workflow.** The model drafts; a named human attests. The attestation step is a recorded act that cannot be delegated to the agent, structurally, not by instruction.
</Step>
<Step>
**Make drafts citable by construction.** Provenance travels with every claim from source to sentence, so review means following pointers rather than re-research ([provenance-carrying grounding](/layers/r11-governance-risk-sovereignty#provenance-carrying-grounding-mechanically)).
</Step>
<Step>
**Run a verification pass before anything leaves.** Citations resolve, figures trace to sources, and the check is a separate process from the drafting agent. This is the step whose absence produced the public failure.
</Step>
<Step>
**Retain the full lineage immutably.** The agent's draft, the human's edits, the attestation, and the per-action logs, kept to the regime's retention rules in the system of record, not in the agent's memory.
</Step>
</Steps>

### What the agent may never do

- <I name="x" /> File, submit, or transmit a formal record autonomously.
- <I name="x" /> Sign, certify, or attest, or generate a record of an attestation that did not happen.
- <I name="x" /> Alter or backdate a record after attestation; corrections are new records with their own accountability.

## The caveat that keeps this honest

Deterministic authorization bounds the blast radius. It does not protect intent formation. One of the three payment protocols was red-teamed successfully with prompt injection operating entirely **within** mandate bounds: the mandate held, and the agent still bought the wrong thing. The gate guarantees the damage cannot exceed the mandate; it says nothing about whether the mandate was exercised for the right reason.

Two defences therefore remain necessary with perfect gates in place: upstream injection defence at the points where untrusted content enters the context, and human confirmation of intent for consequential choices inside the bounds. No gate substitutes for them, which is why the implementation steps above keep a confirmation step even where the rail is already deterministic.

## Find the zones in your enterprise

The four zones are the general case; your estate will have its own instances. Four questions locate them, and one yes is enough:

- <I name="landmark" /> Does a regulator or standard name this function, or rely on its records?
- <I name="exchange" /> Is the action irreversible, or is reversing it a legal or financial event in itself?
- <I name="zap" /> Does the decision move money, change access, actuate equipment, or create a record a third party relies on?
- <I name="shield" /> Is there certified or validated logic already making this decision today?

<Steps>
<Step>
**Sweep the tool inventory.** Every tool an agent can call, classified against the four questions. The gateway's tool registry is the natural source of truth.
</Step>
<Step>
**Write the zone register.** One page: the zoned functions, the deterministic decider for each, and the advisory role agents are permitted. Risk signs it; every layer page in this guide assumes it exists.
</Step>
<Step>
**Wire it into the controls.** Zoned actions map to gateway policy and to the required-controls column of [the autonomy contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract#the-controls-column-is-the-contract), so the register is enforced, not aspirational.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Design consequences

- The zone register is written and signed, not tribal knowledge; it is the document that lets your regulator and your board say yes to everything outside it.
- A use case whose value depends on a model deciding inside a zone is reshaped (model informs, rule decides) or rejected. This kills a class of demo that could never ship.
- Everything outside the zones is negotiable and should be negotiated ambitiously; the zones are what make that ambition defensible.

## Evidence and limits

The zone framing itself is this guide's position, argued from convergent evidence rather than measured outcomes. The payment-stack details are vendor artifacts (Visa, Mastercard, Google), consistent in shape but each self-described. The safety-zone exclusions are standards text (IEC 61511, ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024) and joint agency guidance, the strongest evidence class on this page. The records-zone reading rests on FINRA 24-09 and ESMA's 2024 statement, both of which mandate accountability rather than prescribing tooling; interpretations are still moving, so re-verify against the current notices before relying on them for a filing. The intent-formation caveat rests on one published red-team study; treat it as an existence proof, not a rate. The guardrail-bypass measurements that justify deterministic gates over probabilistic filters are on [the enforcement page](/architecture/enforcement#what-guardrails-are-for).
