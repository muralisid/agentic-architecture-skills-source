An agent needs more than a model. It needs information, tools, permission to act, and a way to confirm the result. This map connects those responsibilities to the enterprise systems and teams that provide them.

An inspection request may involve asset records, images, an agent platform, a work-order API, and a responsible engineer. The layers show where those capabilities live. The seven planes explain who does the work, enforces the rules, records evidence, and improves the system.

## Design the boundaries

- [Protect consequential actions](/architecture/deterministic-zones): access, money, physical safety, and official regulatory records.
- [Identify the agent and its sponsor](/architecture/identity-chain), then [enforce permission outside the model](/architecture/enforcement).
- [Choose autonomy for the task](/architecture/autonomy-contract), with enough supervision capacity to support it.
- [Prepare useful knowledge](/memory), [test changes](/architecture/learning-flywheel), and [check all ten concerns](/architecture/concern-matrix).
- [Examine threats and security practices](/security), including misleading instructions in retrieved material.

## Adapt to the organization

Use the [enterprise reference designs](/library/architecture/master-target-state) to compare starting points. A company operating agents inside purchased software has different controls available from a team operating its own agent platform. Check those interfaces rather than assuming one gateway observes every action.

Business leaders can start with [use cases](/use-cases). Architects can open [all fourteen detailed layers](/layers), or use an [architecture review skill](/skills).
