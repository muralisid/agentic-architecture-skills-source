## The shape

An agentic enterprise is fourteen layers of estate with a seven-plane agent system built across them. The layers are what you already own: infrastructure, data, integration, records, line-of-business systems, and the rest. The planes are what you build: **Execution** (where agent work runs), **Action** (how agents reach your systems), **Knowledge** (what agents know), **Control** (what is allowed), **Improvement** (how behaviour changes), **Evidence** (what you can prove afterward), **Human** (who decides and answers for it).

<GuideFigure id="seven-plane-architecture" fullWidth />

Two rules bind the planes, and they are the two most often violated:

1. **Enforcement lives in the control plane, never in the execution plane.** An instruction in a prompt is a preference. The same rule evaluated at a gateway or policy decision point is a control. Every layer page names where its enforcement actually sits.
2. **The evidence plane is fed by collection the agent cannot influence.** Anything an agent reports about itself is testimony. Traces, decisions, and provenance are collected out of band.

## The two-estate reality

Every design must survive the fact that agent work runs in two estates. The **metered estate** flows through your own gateways: fully governable, budget-enforced, traced on your terms. The **licensed estate** (suite copilots, embedded record-system agents) runs on vendor control planes your gateway never sees: governable only through tenant policy and telemetry extraction, with documented API gaps. Architectures that assume one gateway governs everything are the most common structural error at enterprise scale. Costing that prices agent actions without pricing the governance plane is wrong by a variable amount, because governance is a separate per-user SKU in one stack and free in another [vendor pricing].

## Three builds of the same planes

<GuideFigure id="three-target-architectures" fullWidth />

A globally regulated enterprise runs both estates and doubles nothing else: same knowledge plane, same registry, same evidence machinery, two enforcement surfaces. A mid-market estate rents the control plane from its productivity vendor and builds only the knowledge plane, which vendors do not curate for you. A digital-native estate has the execution and action planes early and must add control and evidence before autonomy rises, which is what the [autonomy contract](/architecture/autonomy-contract) enforces.
