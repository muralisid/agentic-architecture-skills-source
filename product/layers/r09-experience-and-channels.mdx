The experience and channels layer (R09) is where customers and staff meet the organisation. It covers phone, chat, apps, portals, text messages, letters and the screens operators use. It is one of the [fourteen layers](/layers) you own. Today it holds contact centre platforms, phone menus, portals, outage maps and a first wave of chatbots. When an agent talks to a customer, what it says is the company speaking, in law and in public. So this layer must provide a separate customer-facing edge. The edge carries the channels, the AI disclosure, the hand-over to a person and the record of what was said. Everything behind the edge is shared with every other agent. That includes knowledge, identity, tools, testing, monitoring and model access. These are shared platform capabilities, part of the [seven capabilities](/architecture/system-view) an agent platform adds. Measure whether the customer's problem was solved, not whether the conversation avoided a person.

## What agents add

<GuideFigure id="layer-09-hero" fullWidth />

### What the agent says is the company speaking

In Moffatt v Air Canada (2024), a Canadian tribunal rejected the claim that a chatbot is a separate legal entity. It found negligent misrepresentation. A German appeal court (OLG Hamm, 12 May 2026) held that a chatbot is part of corporate communication. Its statements count as the operator's, like an employee's, and general disclaimers do not protect the operator. So failures in front of customers can be pinned on the company, can bind it, and are public. Failures inside the company stay contained and can be put right. That legal exposure is the reason for a separate edge.

### Build only the edge separately

Five things belong to the customer edge, because nothing inside the company does the same job.

- Channels and telephony, including callers who interrupt the agent mid-sentence.
- Measures of containment and escalation, wired to workforce management.
- AI disclosure and consent.
- Guardrails hardened against anonymous attackers. A car dealership's chatbot was talked into agreeing to sell a vehicle for $1.
- A record of what was said, to whom and when, kept as legal evidence.

Everything else is shared with the rest of the organisation's agents. That means the knowledge base, customer identity data, tools, tests, monitoring and model access. Running a second copy of any of them is a defect. Each shared part has its home. Knowledge sits in the [data platform layer (R02)](/layers/r02-data-platform) and the [agent data engineering layer (R14)](/layers/r14-agent-data-engineering). Identity sits in the [security and identity layer (R10)](/layers/r10-security-and-identity). Tools pass through the [integration fabric layer (R03)](/layers/r03-integration-fabric). Tests sit in the [intelligence and learning layer (R06)](/layers/r06-intelligence-and-learning). Monitoring sits in the [observability and FinOps layer (R12)](/layers/r12-observability-and-finops). Model access sits in the [agent platform layer (R07)](/layers/r07-agent-platform).

### Tell customers it is an AI

EU AI Act Article 50(1) became enforceable on 2 August 2026. People must be told they are dealing with an AI system, at the latest at first contact. The exception for when it is obvious is read narrowly (Commission guidelines, 20 July 2026). Penalties reach EUR 15 million or 3 percent of worldwide turnover. The duty applies outside the EU wherever the outputs are used in the EU. The US is a patchwork of state laws, with different triggers in California, Utah and Colorado. The [governance, risk and sovereignty layer (R11)](/layers/r11-governance-risk-sovereignty) tracks these dates. In the EU, the design question is now how to disclose, not whether.

Cursor's support bot (April 2025) invented a login policy to explain logouts caused by a timing bug. Its replies were not labelled as AI, and developers cancelled within hours. The fix labelled every AI reply in email support.

### Measure problems solved

<TeachingIllustration src="/images/layers/r09-customer-journey-labeled-v1.webp" title="A good journey protects identity, keeps choice and measures resolution" alt="Five steps run in order. A conversation that ends without a person counts as contained, whether the customer was helped or gave up. That is false <Term k="containment">containment</Term>. So measure resolution and repeat contact as a pair. Price the cost per resolved issue, not the cost per contact. Klarna reversed course in May 2025 after automating 67 percent of chats [vendor]. Commonwealth Bank cut 45 roles over a voice bot, then called it an error and offered them back. Both had set a containment or headcount target, and resolution did not follow. A real person must be reachable, and the customer should not have to start again. Hand-over with the conversation's state is part of launch, not a later addition.

### Read vendor claims against vendor tests

Salesforce's own research benchmark, CRMArena-Pro (May 2025), reads against its own marketing. The best model scored 58.3 percent on single-turn tasks and 30 to 35 percent on multi-turn ones. The same vendor reports 84 percent resolution in production [vendor]. The task mixes and definitions differ, so this guide reports both. Test on multi-turn conversations, because single-turn tests overstate capability by about half.

### Voice is its own problem

A voice agent lives or dies on delay and on handling interruptions. A chain of speech-to-text, a language model and text-to-speech adds 1 to 3 seconds per turn. Speech-to-speech models cut that delay. Practitioners treat under half a second as natural and over a second as broken. These are thresholds in circulation, not measured results. McDonald's ended its IBM drive-thru partnership in July 2024 over acoustics, not language.

### Screens a model builds

Some agents reply with screens, cards and forms that a model builds at run time. That is a display choice, not a new kind of agent. The [four questions](/architecture/agent-types) about the agent still apply. Four rules apply to any such screen. A fixed check confirms its structure before the customer sees it. It shows the source of each fact. Its buttons come only from a fixed list of permitted actions, enforced outside the model. Personalising it by behaviour or emotion needs the customer's consent. No outside measurement of these screens was found.

### Customers' own agents are arriving

Customers' shopping agents now read company pages and buy through new protocols. The Agentic Commerce Protocol covers checkout, and the Universal Commerce Protocol covers the merchant side. The Agent Payments Protocol carries signed spending permissions, called mandates. Product pages are the least readable to these agents, at 66 percent in one scorecard [vendor]. So make product data readable by machines.

### A worked example: a utility customer account

Take a water utility, or an energy retailer, serving customers by chat and phone. The agent says it is an AI and offers a person. It checks identity with fixed rules, and asks for a one-time code before any change. For an energy retailer, it drafts a home move and sends the market steps through the existing gateway. When a customer mentions life support, a fixed rule registers it at once. For a payment plan, the agent gathers facts and shows options within policy. A trained person approves the plan. When it hears signs of hardship, the agent hands over warmly, with a summary. The hardship officer decides. Payments go through a compliant pay link, and the agent never sees a card number.

Hardship, payment plans and disconnection fall under [the rule for decisions about people](/architecture/deterministic-zones#the-rule-for-decisions-about-people). A named person decides them. Credits, refunds and account changes that move money sit in the money zone of the [deterministic zones](/architecture/deterministic-zones#movement-of-money). There a fixed rule or a named person decides. In Australia, the energy rule maker (AEMC) made a final rule on 10 September 2026. It strengthens payment difficulty and disconnection protections from 30 March 2027. A second rule, on payment plan standards, was still open on 15 October 2026.

## What must be true before an agent touches it

Before an agent faces a customer, these must hold.

- **It says it is an AI.** It tells people at first contact, in every channel, in a clear and accessible way.
- **It knows who it is talking to.** The customer is verified, with a step-up check before any change. The agent acts only within that customer's own entitlements.
- **It answers from approved sources.** It grounds answers in a governed knowledge base. It refuses rather than improvises on policy.
- **A person is reachable.** A staffed queue exists, and the hand-over carries the conversation's state. Containment is never made by closing the human queue.
- **Money and people decisions stay fixed.** Changes that move money pass the money zone. Decisions about a person go to a named person.
- **Card numbers never reach it.** Payments go through a PCI DSS path.
- **The record sits outside the agent.** Transcripts and traces are stored where the agent cannot change them.
- **It is tested like a public statement.** Adversarial tests run before launch and after each change. Its [risk profile](/architecture/risk-profile) is scored before release.

The enforcement point is the tool gateway and the policy decision point, which the edge shares with every other agent. Disclosure is fixed in each channel's configuration, not left to the model. Both follow [enforcement outside the model](/architecture/enforcement).

## Choices and their verdicts

Each choice is the common choice, tested against the simplest credible alternative. The full records are in the [decision catalog](/decisions).

- **[Containment or resolution as the target](/decisions#cd-20) (CD-20):** target resolution, and let containment be an outcome. Every documented reversal had set a containment or headcount target.
- **[A separate customer stack or a shared platform](/decisions#cd-24) (CD-24):** a separate edge on shared platform capabilities. The reason is legal exposure, not technology.

## Concerns this layer must honour

The ten cross-cutting concerns are set out in the [concern matrix](/architecture/concern-matrix). This layer carries a specific duty for eight of them.

- **Identity and access:** customer identity and entitlement checks come before any account action.
- **Observability:** resolution, repeat contact and escalation by trigger, with containment tracked apart.
- **Traceability and audit:** full conversation records are kept as legal evidence.
- **Grounding:** answers come from a governed knowledge base, and the agent refuses rather than improvises on policy.
- **Impersonation:** AI disclosure is mandatory, and no agent presents as a named person.
- **Safety and oversight:** a real queue for escalation, gated actions that cannot be undone, and paths for vulnerable customers.
- **Cost:** cost per resolved issue, not per contact.
- **Resilience:** a defined fallback when no person is available.

## Evidence and limits

The legal case for a separate edge rests on court rulings from February 2024 and May 2026. No published case measures a unified customer and internal stack at scale. None credits success to deliberate separation either. So this guide recommends the separate edge on legal grounds, not measured outcomes.

Rollback is normal. In a vendor-commissioned survey of 2,527 leaders, 74 percent had rolled back an AI customer agent. Each rollback followed a governance failure (Sinch, January to February 2026).

Disclosure has a measured cost and a measured benefit. In a 2019 field experiment, up-front bot disclosure cut purchase rates by more than 79.7 percent. Customers who knew they were dealing with AI reported satisfaction 34 points higher (COPC, January 2026).

Many figures here come from vendors and carry the [vendor] tag. Gartner figures reached this guide second-hand, so confirm the wording before citing them. No credible independent benchmark for escalation rates exists as of mid-2026. The voice delay bands are practitioner thresholds, not measured results.
