## Target state

A separate customer-facing edge on a shared control plane. The edge owns what has no internal analogue: channel and telephony infrastructure, containment and escalation instrumentation wired to workforce management, disclosure and consent mechanics, brand-safety guardrails hardened against anonymous adversaries, and the legal-attribution and evidentiary layer. The knowledge corpus, customer identity data, tool and action layer, evaluation harnesses, observability, and model access are shared with the rest of the estate; duplicating any of them is a defect, not a safeguard. Every statement the agent makes is corporate communication, legally attributable to the operator. The agent grounds in a governed knowledge system, refuses rather than improvises on policy, discloses its nature at first interaction, and escalates to a real human queue with working-state transfer. Resolution and repeat contact are the paired targets; containment is an outcome, never the target. The edge is becoming two-sided: customer agents answer inbound while the enterprise's own pages and checkout must be legible to the customer's agent.

## Mechanisms

### The separate edge, itemized

Edge-specific because no internal analogue exists: telephony acoustics and barge-in; containment and escalation instrumentation wired to workforce management; disclosure and consent mechanics; guardrails built for anonymous, incentivised adversaries (internal agents face authenticated employees under an acceptable-use policy; a Chevrolet dealership assistant was manipulated into agreeing to sell a vehicle for $1 and asserting the offer was legally binding); and the evidentiary layer, because transcripts are legal evidence. Shared because duplication is a defect: knowledge corpus, customer identity data, tool and action layer, eval harnesses, observability, model access. Knowledge is the layer you least want duplicated; Gartner opened an inaugural Customer Service Knowledge Management Magic Quadrant (5 Aug 2026) because knowledge quality is make-or-break. Market structure supports the split: four distinct Gartner CX markets (CCaaS, Sep 2025; Conversational AI, Jul 2026; CRM Customer Engagement Center; CS knowledge management, Aug 2026) whose vendor rosters barely overlap with internal-agent platforms, with Microsoft and ServiceNow appearing in none of the 14 Conversational AI or 9 CCaaS positions; Microsoft is pushing the other way with Service Agent GA in M365 Copilot (15 Jul 2026) unifying customer service across Dynamics and M365. The justification for the separate lane is legal, not technical. Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024 BCCRT 149) rejected the argument that a chatbot is a separate legal entity as "a remarkable submission" and found negligent misrepresentation; the correct-information-was-elsewhere defence failed. OLG Hamm (12 May 2026) held a chatbot is part of corporate communication, not a third party: statements are attributed to the operator like employee statements, liability attaches regardless of training-data provenance, and general disclaimers do not provide sufficient protection. LG Muenchen I (28 May 2026) extended operator liability to AI Overviews about third parties. Customer-facing failures are attributable, contractually binding, and public; internal failures are contained and recoverable.

### Voice architecture

The architecture split is the dividing line: a cascaded STT-LLM-TTS pipeline adds 1 to 3 seconds per turn; speech-to-speech collapses it. Latency bands in circulation among practitioners: under 500 ms reads natural, 500 to 1,000 ms acceptable, over 1,000 ms broken; treat these as thresholds in circulation, not measured distributions. Barge-in, the hard problem with no text or internal analogue, decomposes into three controls that must agree under telephony constraints: VAD sensitivity (is that sound an interruption), STT confidence (was it speech worth acting on), and TTS cancellation (stop playback cleanly mid-utterance). The famous failure was acoustics, not language: McDonald's ended its IBM drive-thru partnership in July 2024 on a beamforming and acoustics problem, and Taco Bell slowed its rollout after viral failures while reporting 2M+ successful orders across 500+ locations [vendor], the signature of a system that works in-distribution and fails visibly out of it. Demand is pull: 97% of 400 surveyed leaders already use voice technology and 80% run some voice agent, but only 21% are very satisfied (Deepgram and Opus Research) [vendor]. No independent production accuracy figures for voice agents exist; published latency distributions come from small vendor samples.

### Advertised versus achieved

CRMArena-Pro (arXiv 2505.18878, May 2025, 4,280 task instances), published by Salesforce's own research arm against its own marketing: the best model scores 58.3% on single-turn B2C tasks and falls to 30.0-35.1% multi-turn; knowledge QA lands around 23-36%; confidentiality awareness is 0-0.4% refusal under standard prompts, rising to 24-62% with hardened prompts at a cost of 1 to 11 points of task success. Contrast the same vendor's production self-report: 84% resolution across 380,000 conversations, support headcount from 9,000 to about 5,000, costs down 17% (Sept 2025) [vendor]. The task mixes and resolution definitions differ; this guide reports both figures and the gap between them. Two design consequences follow: multi-turn is the honest benchmark condition, because single-turn overstates capability by roughly half; and confidentiality behaviour is not free, it is bought with hardened prompts at a measured task-success cost that belongs in the budget.

### Measurement architecture

False containment is the named metric failure mode: a session that ends without escalation scores as contained whether the customer was helped or gave up. The corrective is structural, not analytic: instrument post-contact resolution and repeat-contact rate as a pair, and price cost per resolved issue rather than per contact; circulating vendor pricing of $0.99 to $2.00 per automated resolution against a $6 to $12 human comparator [vendor] means nothing when the denominator counts abandonments. Consumer outcome is a step function on resolution: 74% satisfied with their most recent AI interaction, above 90% when resolved without further steps, NPS down by as much as 70 points on failure, and in the US full resolution followed a failed AI interaction only about half the time (COPC, Jan 2026, 1,000+ consumers, six countries). COPC names three primary causes of AI CX failure: measuring deflection rather than resolution, the people-simulation trap (optimising for sounding human), and weak data foundations. Escalation is where instrumentation is weakest: 52% of Chinese respondents experienced context loss during escalation, only 20% of Australians called the handover seamless, and 74% find retelling their story frustrating (Zendesk CX Trends 2026, 11,000+ respondents, 22 countries) [vendor]. Preference is barely moving: 84.9% of consumers prefer human agents, 80.1% still do even when assured of equal resolution, and 13% prefer AI, up from 11.6% (Metrigy, n=503, fielded Nov 2025); escalation with working-state transfer is therefore launch scope, not a fast follow. No credible independent escalation-rate benchmark exists as of mid-2026; this guide states the absence as a finding rather than repeating circulating figures.

### Disclosure law as a design input

EU AI Act Article 50(1) became enforceable on 2 August 2026 and was not deferred by the omnibus that delayed the high-risk regime: inform people they are interacting with an AI system at the latest at first interaction, in a clear, distinguishable, accessibility-compliant manner; the "unless obvious" exception is judged against an average reasonably well-informed person and is to be interpreted restrictively (Commission guidelines, 20 July 2026); penalties reach EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover; the obligation applies extraterritorially where outputs are used in the EU; Article 50(2) synthetic-content marking has a grace period to 2 December 2026. The US is a patchwork with three different triggers: commerce-triggered (California SB 1001, 2019), request- and sector-triggered (Utah AIPA, 2024), and status-triggered plus duties (Colorado HB 26-1263 Chatbot Safety Act, signed 29 May 2026, effective 1 January 2027). The paradox the law forecloses: a field experiment with more than 6,200 customers found up-front bot disclosure cut purchase rates by more than 79.7% while undisclosed bots matched proficient human agents (Luo, Tong, Fang and Qu, Marketing Science 38(6), 2019), yet customers aware they were interacting with AI reported satisfaction 34 percentage points higher (COPC, Jan 2026). Disclosure destroys persuasion and improves service; Article 50 removes the choice in the EU, so the live design question is how to disclose, not whether. The clearest published control-that-worked came from an incident: Cursor's support bot (April 2025) invented a single-device login policy to explain logouts actually caused by a race condition, responses were not labelled as AI, and developers cancelled within hours; the remediation fixed the bug and labelled all AI responses in email support, adopting the disclosure control fourteen months before Article 50 made it mandatory.

### Agentic commerce

Three protocols with a division of labour: ACP (OpenAI and Stripe, Sep 2025; spec version 2026-04-17, beta) for agent-driven checkout, UCP (Google and Shopify, announced at NRF on 11 January 2026 with Etsy, Wayfair, Target, and Walmart) for the merchant surface, and AP2 for payment authorization mandates. Machine-readability is the binding constraint: Adobe's Q1 2026 scorecard puts product detail pages last at 66% readable, below returns, contact, and FAQ pages at 80%+ [vendor]; the pages that matter most for conversion are the least legible to the customer's agent. Direction reversed within twelve months: AI-referred traffic converted 9% worse in March 2025 and 42% better in March 2026, with traffic up 393% year on year and time on site up 48% (Adobe Analytics, 1T+ visits) [vendor]; Salesforce reported AI and agents influencing $67B, 20% of global orders, across Cyber Week 2025 [vendor]. Access is contested and currently favours the agent: the Ninth Circuit overturned Amazon's injunction against Perplexity's Comet, holding Amazon unlikely to succeed on its CFAA claim because Amazon's own users, not Perplexity, accessed the platform through the agentic browser. Whether agentic access can lawfully be refused remains unsettled.

## Design decisions

- **Containment-as-target versus resolution-as-target** ([CD-20](/decisions#cd-20)): set a resolution target and let containment be an outcome; the AI-first versus human-first framing is the wrong axis. Every documented reversal set a containment or headcount target and found resolution did not follow: Klarna reversed in May 2025 after automating 67% of chats (2.3M in 30 days, the work of 700 FTE [vendor]), its CEO conceding cost-cutting was overweighted; Commonwealth Bank cut 45 roles citing a voice bot reducing calls by 2,000 per week, then admitted volumes were rising, called the redundancy an "error" (21 Aug 2025), and offered the roles back; McDonald's ended the IBM drive-thru partnership in July 2024. Gartner's forecasts converge from four directions (via relay): 80% autonomous resolution of common issues by 2029, yet 50% of organisations expecting service-workforce cuts abandoning those plans by 2027, no Fortune 500 company fully eliminating human customer service by 2028, and half of AI-citing staff cutters rehiring by 2027, while only about a fifth had actually cut staffing. The strongest independent causal evidence sits on the assist side: 5,172 support agents, +15% issues resolved per hour, largest gains for novices, better customer sentiment and agent retention (Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, QJE 140(2), May 2025); nothing on the deflect side has comparable standing.
- **Separate stack versus shared platform for customer-facing agents** ([CD-24](/decisions#cd-24)): separate lane and separate edge on a shared control plane. The lane is justified legally, not technically: customer-facing failures are legally attributable, contractually binding, and publicly visible, which warrants different governance, funding gates, and risk appetite regardless of which platform the agent runs on. Honest caveat: no published enterprise case measures a deliberately unified customer and internal stack at scale, and none attributes success to deliberate separation either; the verdict rests on market structure and risk profile, not measured architecture outcomes.

## Cross-cutting concerns

| # | Concern | Treatment at this layer |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Identity and access | Customer identity and entitlement checks before any account action; the agent acts within the customer's own scope |
| C2 | Observability | Resolution, repeat contact, escalation by trigger; containment tracked separately from resolution |
| C3 | Traceability and audit | Full conversation records kept as legal evidence: what was said, to whom, when |
| C4 | Grounding | Governed knowledge base rather than model memory; refusal over improvisation on policy |
| C5 | Impersonation | Mandatory AI disclosure; no agent presents as a named human |
| C6 | Sovereignty | Conversation-data residency; recording and consent law by jurisdiction |
| C7 | Privacy | Customer-data minimisation in prompts and logs; consent for training use |
| C8 | Safety and oversight | Escalation to a real queue; irreversible actions gated; vulnerable-customer paths |
| C9 | Cost | Cost per resolved issue, not per contact; outcome pricing understood before signing |
| C10 | Resilience | Defined fallback when no human is available; containment never manufactured through queue unavailability |

## Evidence and limits

The incident record is dated: the Chevrolet $1-vehicle manipulation; Moffatt v. Air Canada decided February 2024 (2024 BCCRT 149); the McDonald's and IBM drive-thru termination, July 2024; the Cursor support-bot incident and remediation, April 2025; the Klarna reversal, May 2025; the Commonwealth Bank reversal, August 2025; OLG Hamm, 12 May 2026; LG Muenchen I, 28 May 2026. No CVEs attach to this layer's record. Rollback is normal rather than exceptional: 74% of 2,527 senior decision-makers across ten countries had already rolled back or shut down an AI customer-communications agent after a governance failure, rising to 81% among organisations with mature guardrails (maturity correlates with catching failures, not avoiding them), and 76% invest more in trust, security, and compliance than in AI development itself (Sinch, Jan-Feb 2026, vendor-commissioned, fielded by an independent institute).

Vendor-published figures are flagged [vendor] inline: the 84%-resolution self-report, the Deepgram and Opus voice survey, the Taco Bell order counts, the Zendesk escalation figures, the Adobe traffic and readability series, the Cyber Week aggregate, and the per-resolution price ranges. Gartner figures reached this guide via relay because the primary press releases were unavailable at research time; confirm wording before citing onward. The voice latency bands are practitioner thresholds in circulation, not measured distributions. The legal-not-technical justification for the separate lane, and the reading that disclosure destroys persuasion while improving service, are the authors' synthesis of the cited cases and studies.

Refusals: a widely recycled pairing of two vendors' containment figures was traced to its attributed article and the figures are not in it; this guide treats the pairing as fabricated in transit and excludes it. Also excluded as unverifiable: circulating figures for voice-agent penetration among large banks, year-on-year voice-deployment growth, an average containment rate, every escalation-rate benchmark, and a pilots-never-reach-production rate. Re-verify on a quarterly cycle: Article 50 enforcement practice and first penalties, the Colorado act's 1 January 2027 effective date, ACP and UCP specification status (both pre-1.0), the agentic-access line of cases after the Ninth Circuit ruling, and whether a credible independent escalation benchmark has appeared.
