Agents do not arrive on a blank page. They sit on technology a company already runs, from its servers to the people who supervise the work. This guide splits that estate into fourteen layers you own. Every agent passes through [a lifecycle you run](/architecture/agent-lifecycle), from registration to retirement. Each stage of that lifecycle calls on [seven capabilities you add](/architecture/system-view), such as a tool gateway and an audit trail. Agents touch all fourteen layers. Find the layer you run in the groups below, and read its page.

## Run the agents

| Layer | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| [Infrastructure and compute (R01)](/layers/r01-infrastructure) | Each agent session is sealed off, with one guarded exit and its own identity. Model capacity is rented by default |
| [Agent platform (R07)](/layers/r07-agent-platform) | Buy the runtime that runs agents. Build your own code around the model, and test every change before release |

## Let them act

| Layer | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| [Integration fabric (R03)](/layers/r03-integration-fabric) | One tool gateway checks every agent request, supplies the credentials and keeps the record |
| [Systems of record (R04)](/layers/r04-systems-of-record) | Agents act as the person who asked. The business system keeps its own permission checks |
| [Line of business and OT (R05)](/layers/r05-lob-and-ot) | Agents advise the people who run equipment. They never control it |

## Give them knowledge

| Layer | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| [Data platform (R02)](/layers/r02-data-platform) | Agents find only what the person asking may open, and every answer shows its source |
| [Agent data engineering (R14)](/layers/r14-agent-data-engineering) | Data is prepared for agents with an owner and a source on every piece. Deletion reaches every copy |

## Control and check them

| Layer | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| [Security and identity (R10)](/layers/r10-security-and-identity) | Every agent has its own identity. A fixed rule outside the model checks each risky action |
| [Governance, risk and sovereignty (R11)](/layers/r11-governance-risk-sovereignty) | A register of every agent, records kept to match the risk, and data kept where the law requires |
| [Observability and FinOps (R12)](/layers/r12-observability-and-finops) | A trace of every step that the agent cannot alter, and a budget it cannot exceed |

## Improve them

| Layer | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| [Intelligence and learning (R06)](/layers/r06-intelligence-and-learning) | Experts write the tests. Model judges are checked against people. A change ships only after it passes, and can be rolled back |

## Work with people

| Layer | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| [Productivity and collaboration (R08)](/layers/r08-productivity-and-collaboration) | Office agents act with their own agent identity. A user account that joins meetings and chats is granted rarely |
| [Experience and channels (R09)](/layers/r09-experience-and-channels) | Agents that talk to customers get their own front door, because what they say carries legal weight |
| [Operating model and supervision (R13)](/layers/r13-operating-model) | Supervisor capacity is planned like a queue, counting waiting time and the busiest ten minutes |

## How the pages are built

Every layer page follows the same order.

1. An opening paragraph: what the layer is, what sits in it today, and what agents need from it.
2. What sits in this layer today. A table shows the systems in four kinds of organisation. They are an electricity distributor, a water utility, a hospital network, and a generator or miner.
3. What agents add to the layer.
4. What must be true before an agent touches it, and where that rule is enforced.
5. Choices and their verdicts, each linked to the [decision catalog](/decisions).
6. The concerns this layer must honour, linked to the [concern matrix](/architecture/concern-matrix). A concern is a requirement every layer must honour, such as identity, privacy or cost.
7. Evidence and limits: what the sources support and where they stop.
8. A next step, and the research behind the page with its dated sources.

The research for each layer, with findings and sources, is in [the research library](/library/layers).

## Evidence and limits

The list of fourteen layers is this guide's own. No standards body publishes one. The Cloud Security Alliance's MAESTRO model has seven layers of an agentic system (6 February 2025). That is a different list, about the agent system itself, not the enterprise around it.

The systems named in each layer table come from industry knowledge and three research briefs written on 15 October 2026. Vendor names are examples, not a market-share claim.
