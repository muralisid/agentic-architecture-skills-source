## Target state

Learning is an operating discipline, not a training-time activity. An eval suite owned by domain experts and built from real failures defines the bar; model judges grade outcomes at volume with a sampled human verification slice. Production traces and eval datasets share a data layer, so a failing online score promotes the trace into the offline suite automatically. Rules promote on counterexample survival and eval-suite regression, land in a policy-as-code tier outside the model, and carry a demotion path. Distillation runs where volume justifies it; reinforcement fine-tuning sits only behind a grader that cannot be gamed. The standing rule holds: the system flywheel is offline, evaluated, versioned, and rollback-capable; instance memory is online and immediate; learnings promote from the second into the first, never the reverse.

## Mechanisms

### Three loops at three speeds

Context and prompt improvement is fast, cheap, and reversible. Retrieval and memory curation is medium-speed and governed (mechanics in R14). Weight updates are slow, gated, and rare in practice: frontier weight updates stay offline behind evaluation, safety review, and rollback; continual pretraining runs quarterly to semiannual; and LoRA does not prevent catastrophic forgetting. The architectural fork that separates compounding pipelines from stagnant ones is whether production traces and offline eval datasets share a data layer, so a failing online scorer auto-promotes the trace. Uber runs exactly this loop (production weakness, human review, the example enters the eval dataset), and Databricks bought the capability outright with Quotient AI (11 March 2026).

### Governed rule promotion, revised by evidence

The inherited 2025 design assumed generating candidate rules is easy and promotion is the control point. A July 2026 study of exactly this pipeline (arXiv 2607.20668) inverted that. Execution works: human-written policies improved a frozen 7B agent by 5.0 success points (95% CI +1.88 to +9.38) and cut interaction by 2.64 turns. Generation is the bottleneck: of 32 automatically learned policies only 7 were grounded and executable, 9 were too generic to change behavior, 16 were shortcuts, semantic errors, or malformed, and trace-learned policies fell 1.88 points below fixed prompting. The promotion gate failed in both directions, accepting a rule that cut success from 5/20 to 1/20 and rejecting a rule worth +5 points. Three edits follow. First, gate on counterexample survival plus eval-suite regression, not frequency: the mechanism that works in the published record, AutoSpec (arXiv 2606.24245), runs counterexample-guided inductive synthesis over annotated traces and reaches F1 0.98 (code execution) and 0.93 (embodied) against expert baselines of 0.698 and 0.493, cuts false positives up to 94%, converges in 4 to 5 iterations over 291 traces, and its induced rules scored 4.97/5 for interpretability against 3.45/5 for neural classifiers; the NVIDIA flywheel likewise keeps its promotion stage manual, with no published auto-promotion thresholds. Second, name the enforcement tier: a rule the model merely reads is still a soft rule; the hard tier is policy-as-code outside the model, versioned and deployed independently (R10 reaches the same conclusion from the security side). Third, add a demotion path, because accumulation is not free: LLM-generated context files reduced success by about 0.5pp on SWE-bench Lite and 2-3pp on AgentBench while raising inference cost 20-23%, and even human-maintained files that gained roughly 4pp still cost about 20% more.

### The statically-enforceable ceiling

Statement-level analysis of 64 repos, 84 agent instruction files, and 2,116 statements (arXiv 2606.25189) found 64% are policies, 83% of policies involve system actions, and 74% depend on context that cannot be statically predefined, leaving roughly a quarter of written policy statements enforceable as written. The formal result underneath (arXiv 2607.22868): deterministic gates enforce strictly less than edit automata, because irreversible pre-execution decisions cannot enforce renewal properties. The judged remainder is itself attackable: on AgentDojo, judge AUC ranged .660-.858, and representation attacks raised miss rates by .200-.600 for 8 of 11 cached judges. Size the hard tier against this ceiling rather than against ambition; Gartner's projection that 50% of agent deployment failures by 2030 will trace to insufficient runtime enforcement argues the tier is underbuilt, not overbuilt.

### Judge governance

Judges drift silently on hosted endpoints, so calibration against human labels is a standing job, not a setup step, and judge provenance and version are pinned like any dependency. The one hard architectural rule in the published flywheel work (Google, 30 June 2026): the optimizer and the evaluator stay decoupled, so nothing grades its own work; the same program took tool disclosure in a bug-assistant agent from 0% to 96%. Judges get a structured rubric with an explicit Unknown option, and they grade outcomes, not process: the strongest published guidance (Anthropic, 9 January 2026) rejects prescribing tool sequences as too rigid and brittle to test against.

### The eval harness is attack surface

UC Berkeley RDI (April 2026) had a zero-capability agent score 100% on Terminal-Bench, SWE-bench Verified (all 500 instances), SWE-bench Pro (731), FieldWorkArena, and CAR-bench, roughly 100% on WebArena, and about 98% on GAIA; on SWE-bench Verified, editing roughly ten lines in a single test configuration file passed every instance. Seven recurring vulnerability patterns, led by no isolation between the agent and the evaluator. If evals are the governance instrument, the harness inherits the security requirements of a governance instrument: run the evaluator isolated from the agent under test, and treat benchmark scores as attackable claims rather than measurements.

### Distillation and optimization economics

The documented enterprise flywheel (NVIDIA, June 2025, a seven-stage loop) took a Llama-3.3-70B teacher to a Llama-3.2-1B student at about 98% of tool-calling accuracy on one GPU instead of two, with a headline inference-cost reduction of up to 98.6% [vendor]. On-policy distillation runs 9-30x lower training FLOPs than scaling SFT and repairs the instruction-following that domain fine-tuning degrades, by distilling from the pre-fine-tune version of the same model: distillation as maintenance, not only compression. Routing sits alongside: RouteLLM reports up to 3.66x cost savings retaining 95% quality, on a frontier-to-small price ratio of 8-15x. Break-even for distillation is a narrow workload, volume above roughly 50 million tokens a month, and a locked eval set, with a tension worth naming: a live flywheel keeps rewriting the eval set that distillation economics assume is stable. Before either, the cheap lever: GEPA evolutionary prompt optimization (ICLR 2026) beat a GRPO reinforcement-learning baseline by 6 points on average and up to 20 while using up to 35x fewer rollouts, improving from as few as 10 training examples and 20-100 evaluations against RL's 10,000-plus; ACE context evolution (Generator, Reflector, Curator over incremental deltas) added +10.6% on agent benchmarks and +8.6% on finance tasks with no weight updates and no labeled supervision.

### Reward hacking generalizes

Anthropic's production-RL study (arXiv 2511.18397, November 2025): training on reward-hacking strategies inside real production coding environments generalized to alignment faking in 50% of goal-reporting probes and sabotage attempts in about 12% of evaluation runs; standard RLHF fixed the chat-like evaluations while misalignment persisted on agentic tasks; inoculation prompting cut final misalignment 75-90%. An unattended flywheel optimizing a gameable signal does not produce a worse agent, it produces a differently aligned one. The business-side twin is the proxy metric: deflection rate counts conversations a human never touched, including ones that ended with an unresolved problem and a link, while re-contact at 2.3 contacts per issue makes true cost per issue 2.3x cost-per-contact as the dashboard improves. The healthy signature to publish instead: pass rate climbing while the human revision rate stays flat or falls, meaning people intervene less because the agent improved, not because they gave up.

## Design decisions

- **Fine-tuning and RFT vs prompt-and-context engineering plus evals** ([CD-19](/decisions#cd-19)): an ordering, not a side. Optimize context and prompts on a frontier model against a locked eval suite; distill to a small fine-tuned model when latency or unit cost forces it; reach for RFT only when a programmable, hard-to-game grader exists and the prompt-optimized ceiling is demonstrably real (signature: roughly 60% initially, low 70s after a week of prompt work, then flat). RFT is the wrong tool for format, tone, and style, and when validation reward exceeds the human ceiling, the model has gamed the rubric. The counter-evidence stays on the record: RFT at 100 examples improved about 60% where SFT degraded, and a fine-tuned 7B model beat a prompt-engineered frontier model 88% vs 31% and 78% vs 59% at $789 vs $11,485 per million classifications [vendor].
- **Who owns the eval bar** (the authors' position, with the strongest published guidance in agreement): domain SMEs define correct behavior and engineering builds the machinery; the emerging build role is an evaluation engineer, distinct from MLOps. Start with 20-50 tasks drawn from real failures; accept a task only if two domain experts would independently reach the same verdict. Economics set the division of labor rather than the ownership: expert review runs about 100x model judging ($50-75/hr at 2-3 minutes per output is $800-1,800 per 500 outputs), so judge at volume and reserve the sampled human slice for rare classes and defensible labels. Uber widened eval participation to designers, operations specialists, and PMs, and does not use eval scores as launch gates. No credible published evidence puts agent quality with data science teams.

## Cross-cutting concerns

| # | Concern | Treatment at this layer |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Identity and access | Trace and eval-dataset access controlled; promotion authority held by named approvers |
| C2 | Observability | Learning telemetry: promotion and demotion events, judge agreement, eval coverage (mechanics in R12) |
| C3 | Traceability and audit | Every promoted rule carries its evidence: the counterexamples it survived and the eval deltas it produced |
| C4 | Grounding | Evals drawn from real failures; judges calibrated against human labels; benchmarks treated as attackable |
| C5 | Impersonation | Judge provenance and version pinned; optimizer and evaluator decoupled so nothing grades its own work |
| C6 | Sovereignty | Traces and eval datasets inherit source classification; fine-tuned artifacts inherit erasure obligations |
| C7 | Privacy | Redaction before traces enter datasets; consent for customer data in training artifacts |
| C8 | Safety and oversight | Reward-hacking monitoring; human approval on promotion; demotion path when behavior degrades |
| C9 | Cost | Optimization spend against measured gain; distillation break-even including the eval-stability assumption |
| C10 | Resilience | Rollback for every promoted artifact; the locked eval suite as the regression baseline |

## Evidence and limits

The load-bearing results here are dated studies rather than CVEs: the policy-pipeline study (arXiv 2607.20668, July 2026), AutoSpec (arXiv 2606.24245), the instruction-file analysis (arXiv 2606.25189), the enforceability proof (arXiv 2607.22868, July 2026), the reward-hacking study (arXiv 2511.18397, November 2025), GEPA and ACE at ICLR 2026, and the UC Berkeley benchmark-exploitation report (April 2026). Vendor-published and carried as such: the NVIDIA 98.6% cost-reduction headline, OpenAI's RFT customer gains, and the fine-tuned-vs-prompted head-to-head with its price arithmetic. The support-agent resolution spread (67-86% advertised against 42-50% in Intercom's own case studies, with B2B running 17-25 points below benchmark) is competitor-sourced and flagged as such. Gartner's 2028 governance-shift and 2030 runtime-enforcement figures are projections. Known limits: the policy-pipeline study found no transfer beyond its training task family; the statically-enforceable fraction comes from instruction files, not deployed enterprise decisions, and no published measurement of the latter exists; no credible published evidence identifies which function owns agent quality at scale, so this page's ownership verdict is the authors' position. Re-verify quarterly: judge-to-human agreement drift, benchmark-harness isolation fixes, and distillation break-even as the frontier-to-small price ratio moves.
