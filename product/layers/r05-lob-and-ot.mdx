The line of business and OT layer (R05) runs the physical work. OT, operational technology, means the systems that control physical equipment. This layer holds grid and plant control, alarms, meters, field crews and hospital department systems. It is one of the [fourteen layers](/layers). Agents here watch alarms, tidy asset records and find past cases for a crew. They never control equipment. For agents this layer must provide three things. Agents get a read-only copy of plant data. Every suggestion passes a simulation check. Advice travels in its own channel, apart from alarms.

## Agents advise, people and fixed systems control

Agents sit on the information path, never the control path. The information path carries data and advice to people. The control path carries commands to equipment. A person, or a certified safety system, keeps control of anything physical.

Anything that moves physical equipment is in the safety actuation zone, one of the four [deterministic zones](/architecture/deterministic-zones#safety-actuation). In that zone only fixed rules and named people decide. Any exception is a separate engineering decision, with its own safety case.

## What agents add

Agents bring context an operator cannot hold in their head. They read alarms alongside history, reconcile asset data across systems that disagree, and find past cases for the crew. Here the tool gateway, one of [the seven capabilities an agent platform provides](/architecture/system-view), meets the OT boundary.

### Four patterns

Four patterns describe what agents can do in this layer. They differ in how much evidence stands behind them.

- **Pattern A: alarm advice in the control room.** The agent reads the alarm stream from SCADA, the supervisory control system, and suggests what to check. Vendors already ship the suggestion half. The part worth designing is the validation loop below.
- **Pattern B: a compliance checking agent.** It would check records against grid security rules. It is a design proposal only, with no published example. Sector adoption of compliance automation is about 2 percent. The agent would also fall under those security rules itself.
- **Pattern C: a safety coach for crews.** It would bring relevant past incidents to a crew before a job. No production example exists. The nearest work is a 2026 benchmark study whose authors make no outcome claims.
- **Pattern D: an asset data quality loop.** The agent finds asset records that disagree and proposes fixes. A person approves each change to the asset register. This is the strongest pattern, and the only one with a measured result.

Pattern D is a [learning flywheel](/architecture/learning-flywheel): each approved fix improves the data the next check uses. Its key design decision is the approval step for register changes. Asset changes flow into the regulated asset value, ISO 55001 evidence and regulatory reports. Capturing better data in the field is a change to how people work and are rewarded. No AI feature does that part.

### The validation loop

The operator never sees the model's raw answer. The US National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) published eGridGPT in May 2024. It is a reference design for a grid control room. Staff from two US grid operators, CAISO and ERCOT, reviewed it. The model suggests possible actions. A simulation of the grid, called a digital twin, tests each one. Unsafe options are removed. The operator sees only the options that survive, and decides.

Build this loop before you build the suggestions, because the suggestion half already ships. Honeywell's Alarm Guidance, built with Chevron, shipped on 1 October 2024 [vendor]. A pilot at TotalEnergies Port Arthur gave operators 12 minutes' notice of pressure problems (November 2025) [vendor].

### The alert channel

<TeachingIllustration src="/images/layers/r05-advice-alarm-labeled-v1.webp" alt="Two rows. Real safety alarms are an independent protection, the highest priority, and never replaced by AI. Agent output goes in the alert channel, kept visually apart from real alarms. EEMUA 191 Edition 4 (November 2024) is the Engineering Equipment and Materials Users Association's alarm management guide. It defines an alert as information the operator need not respond to, but can. That channel is the right home for agent advice.

Agent advice never counts as a safety protection. A protective alarm needs a defined proof test. An output that can change from run to run cannot pass one. The channel also has load limits. In steady state, aim for at most one alarm per operator every 10 minutes. In a major upset, aim for at most 10 in the first 10 minutes. At BP-Husky Toledo in September 2022, 3,712 alarms sounded in 12 hours. Two workers died. The US Chemical Safety Board named the alarm flood as a contributing factor (June 2024).

### The measured economic result

One measured result exists, and it is a data task. NREL's eGridGPT converted grid models from the control room format to the planning format in under a minute. Regional staff had needed several weeks for the same job. The result met NERC's model validation standard, MOD-033. A specialist still checks it. So value here is counted in specialist hours, avoided field visits and deferred replacement, not in tokens. Data access holds progress back. EPRI, the US power industry's research institute, puts about 95 percent of utility data behind security and privacy walls.

### One narrow AI does run plant control

Some AI already controls plants. Yokogawa's FKDPP is a reinforcement learning agent, a model trained by trial and reward. It runs direct control at two named plants. At ENEOS Materials, it was adopted permanently in March 2023 after an 840-hour trial. Steam and carbon dioxide fell 40 percent [vendor]. At Aramco's Fadhili gas plant, it was commissioned in phases from October 2024 to April 2025. Amine and steam use fell 10 to 15 percent [vendor].

It earned that place in a specific way. It has a small, bounded set of actions on a well-understood unit. It was trained on a detailed simulator, never the live plant, and tested before commissioning. The independent safety system was left untouched. It is not a language model. Language model agents stay on the information path. Closed-loop AI control is a separate engineering discipline, with its own safety case.

Vendors are now testing that line. Honeywell's Experion Cognition (9 June 2026) says it will "proactively act on behalf of the operator" [vendor]. It runs as a live proof of concept at Borouge's Ruwais plant. It sits inside the control vendor's own system, so like FKDPP it needs its own safety case. DataRobot and Chevron (2 June 2026) aim to replace per-mission permit approval for robot inspections with continuous checks [vendor]. Cognite and ABB (21 May 2026) say their agents can "trigger cross-system actions" [vendor]. None of the three says what its agents may not touch. On this site, control of equipment and of work permits stays with a person.

### The parts and where they run

| Part | What it does | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|
| Validation loop | Tests each suggestion in a simulation or against rules before display | The control room estate, beside the energy or distribution management system |
| Alert channel | Carries agent advice to the operator, apart from alarms | The alarm management system and operator screens |
| OT read broker | Serves copies of plant data to agents, with logs and a kill switch | The buffer zone (DMZ) between plant and office networks |
| One-way path | Sends cleaned plant data out, with nothing coming back | The OT boundary, as a data diode or push design |
| Local model runtime | Runs open-weight models with no public network access | Inside the operator's perimeter |
| Asset change gate | Sends proposed register fixes to a person for approval | The asset management system |
| Revert-to-manual path | Returns control to people or fixed systems, and switches AI features off | The control system estate |

## What must be true before an agent touches it

### The OT boundary: four principles

Nine national cyber agencies published joint guidance on 3 December 2025. Its title is "Principles for the Secure Integration of AI in Operational Technology". It explicitly covers AI agents. The agencies come from the US, Australia, Canada, Germany, the UK, the Netherlands and New Zealand. It sets four principles.

- Keep the ability to go back to manual or fixed control.
- Prefer one-way or push-based data paths, so OT networks stay separate.
- Send cleaned OT data out to separate AI systems. Do not put models inside safety-critical loops.
- Require a parts list for AI components, and a way to switch AI features off.

Ofgem, the UK energy regulator, adds two warnings in its May 2026 AI guidance. It names shutdowns that cascade. It asks for safety-style guardrails and for training operators to spot faults.

### Existing standards still apply

No grid or industrial security standard has an AI part yet. The existing rules still cover any agent that touches regulated systems. IEC 62443 treats the agent as a new kind of actor in a network zone. NERC CIP, the North American grid security rule set, captures agents by what they touch. A compliance agent that reads protected grid information falls under CIP-011 itself. Its model and settings become a change-managed baseline under CIP-010. Inside the protected network perimeter, CIP-005 and CIP-007 apply. That is one reason the published control-room work runs open-weight models locally.

### Two human-factors failure modes

Good advice makes people check less. When decision support is right about 85 percent of the time or more, operators check 40 to 60 percent less. So watch the checking rate beside the acceptance rate.

One confident answer can also narrow what a crew notices. Operators take in handover notes, sounds, vibration, smells and site conditions. A search agent that shows one confident matching case can crowd those out. It can anchor a crew on the last accident.

NERC said in November 2024 that the operator should have the final say on AI-generated actions. Ofgem asks for human oversight designed in from the start. It must allow for both overconfidence and distrust. Ofgem does not require a person in the loop. Its rules are based on outcomes.

### The controls

- **Reads go through a broker or a one-way path.** If agent output could ever influence a control action, the path is one-way and physically enforced.
- **Each agent has its own identity and the least access it needs.** Access can be cut in seconds. Plant assets without a registered name cannot be reached. The identity rules come from [security and identity (R10)](/layers/r10-security-and-identity).
- **The operator holds the logs.** Session logs are kept by the operator, not the vendor.
- **Control-system data stays inside the perimeter.** Where it may not leave, models run locally with no public network access.
- **Going back to manual is documented and tested.** The time it takes is measured. AI features can be switched off.
- **Agent failure cannot weaken the control system.** Field agents also keep working when the network is poor.
- **A person approves every asset register change.** Each proposed fix shows its source. Changes follow CIP-010 and ISO 55001 change management.

The enforcement points are the read broker and the one-way path. Both sit outside the model. What a model reads can steer its behaviour. So behaviour cannot be the security control (Zentera, July 2026) [vendor]. This is [enforcement outside the model](/architecture/enforcement).

## Choices and their verdicts

The entry below is the common choice, tested against the simplest credible alternative. The full record is in the [decision catalog](/decisions).

- [A brokered read path or a one-way path](/decisions#cd-18): it depends on where the agent's output can go. If output can reach or influence a control action, use a one-way path and a tested way back to manual. If output stays in alerts, suggestions and data findings, a broker is enough. The agent's impact decides, one of [the four questions](/architecture/agent-types). The transport comes after that decision.

## Concerns this layer must honour

A concern is a requirement every layer must honour. This layer carries a specific duty for eight of the ten.

- **Identity and access.** Each agent has its own identity and least access. Access can be cut in seconds.
- **Traceability and audit.** Every option shown is tagged with its source, agent or operator, and a time. Proposed fixes show their source.
- **Grounding.** Enforced here by the simulation check before display. Data carries its history.
- **Impersonation and authenticity.** Agent advice cannot pass itself off as an alarm. It stays in the alert channel.
- **Sovereignty and residency.** Control-system data usually may not leave the perimeter. Local open-weight models are the published pattern.
- **Privacy.** Data about field workers, such as location and performance, carries worker protections.
- **Safety and oversight.** Enforced here. The operator holds decision authority, and advice never counts as protection.
- **Resilience.** Agent failure must not weaken the control system. Going back to manual is tested.

The full grid of concerns against layers is the [concern matrix](/architecture/concern-matrix).

## Evidence and limits

The validation loop rests on a national laboratory report and dated vendor releases. The four principles rest on joint guidance from nine agencies. The guidance's exact wording comes from secondary coverage, because the primary page returned access errors. Treat quoted phrasing as unverified until the source document is checked.

Adoption figures come from a NERC survey with 47 respondents (November 2024). Vendor figures are marked [vendor].

Keeping language model agents off the control path is a design rule, not a regulation. Ofgem does not require a person in the loop. FERC has issued no guidance on AI agents in utility operations. No published alarm-triage agent runs in production in an electric utility control room. No agent holds autonomous control at any named water utility. This guide recommends the information-path rule for every language model agent.
