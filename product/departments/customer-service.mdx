## Tuesday, 9:00, the service floor

The morning huddle reads different numbers now. Overnight the resolution agent closed the routine half of chat: order status, returns inside policy, address changes, all inside each customer's own entitlements, all disclosed as AI, all logged verbatim. The eleven conversations that needed a person were escalated with working state attached, so nobody asked the customer to repeat themselves. Agent-assist drafted answers all day yesterday for the humans on the hard queue, which is quietly the highest-return agent in the department. The wall shows resolution and repeat-contact, not containment, because a conversation that ends without help is a failure wearing a good number. All of it deployable now.

Here is the whole plan.

## Start with these two use cases

1. **Agent-assist for your human team.** Drafts, retrieval, and next-best-action for the people on the queue. First because it is consistently the highest-return application in the department, the least demoed, and it cannot embarrass you in public.
2. **End-to-end resolution on a narrow intent set.** Five intents, well-instrumented, recoverable, inside the customer's own scope. Second, and only with escalation to a real staffed queue wired before launch.

Deliberately absent: an AI-first program with a headcount target. Every documented reversal of one set exactly that target.

## The plan, step by step

1. **Choose resolution as the target metric and write it down.** Containment is an outcome you observe, never a goal you set. Done when the dashboard leads with resolution and repeat-contact.
2. **Ship agent-assist to the whole team.** Done when handle time falls with quality assurance stable.
3. **Ground the knowledge base and appoint its owner.** The agent answers from governed knowledge, never from model memory. Done when weak-evidence questions produce escalation, not improvisation.
4. **Ship five intents end to end.** Disclosure on (it is the law in force since August 2026), identity checked before any account action, irreversible actions gated. Done when resolution holds above your human baseline for those intents and repeat-contact does not rise.
5. **Wire the vulnerable-customer path** that bypasses the agent entirely. Done when it is tested monthly.
6. **Expand intent by intent,** each with its own eval set, each reversible on a bad week.

## Worked example: the refund that stayed within bounds

A customer asks for a refund on a delayed order. The agent verifies identity, checks the order against policy, sees the amount is inside its mandate, issues the refund, discloses itself, and records the conversation verbatim. Same conversation, but the order is contested and the amount is above threshold: the agent assembles the history, drafts the options, and hands a warm summary to a human with the customer never re-explaining. The courts have already ruled a company is responsible for what its chatbot says, and disclaimers do not save you; the mandate boundary and the warm handoff are what do.

## Your agent roster

| Agent | What it does | Autonomy |
|---|---|---|
| Agent-assist | Drafts and retrieves for humans on the queue | Suggests only |
| Intent and triage agent | Classifies, retrieves customer context, routes | Executes routing |
| Resolution agent | Handles the narrow intent set end to end | Executes within mandate; escalates |
| Account action agent | Bounded account changes in the customer's scope | Reversible only; irreversible gated |

## What stays human

Relationship repair, ambiguity, vulnerability, and every irreversible commitment. The escalation queue is not a failure mode; it is the product working.

## The economics

Cost per resolved issue, not per contact. Outcome-priced vendors list roughly one to two dollars per automated resolution against six to twelve for a human contact; take the economics, but track repeat-contact beside it, because re-contact multiplies true cost while the dashboard improves.

## Watchouts

- 74% of surveyed decision-makers have already rolled back a customer AI agent after a governance failure, and the rate is higher among those with mature guardrails. The lesson is target-setting, not technology.
- Statements bind: a tribunal held the company responsible for its chatbot, and a 2026 appellate decision held that disclaimers do not shield you.
- Never manufacture containment through queue unavailability. If the human queue is unstaffed, the agent is not resolving; it is trapping.
