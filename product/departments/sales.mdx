## Thursday, 8:45, pipeline review

Every rep walks in with account context they did not compile: the research agent assembled each account's news, usage signals, history, and open threads overnight, with sources shown. The CRM is clean because nobody typed updates; the hygiene agent proposed structured field changes from yesterday's calls and the reps approved them from their phones. The forecast meeting runs on explanations, not archaeology: the analysis agent flags which deals moved, why, and which commit claims disagree with the activity record. The inbound queue was worked all night inside its mandate, and two qualified meetings are on calendars. Nobody's discount was decided by a machine. All of it deployable now.

Here is the whole plan.

## Start with these two use cases

1. **Account research and CRM hygiene.** Context assembled per account; structured updates proposed from calls and mail, rep approves. First because administrative time is the documented sink, the work is solitary, and the failure is a wrong field, not a wrong promise.
2. **Inbound qualification.** Engage inbound enquiries, qualify against your stated criteria, route and book. Second because it is measurable in response time and qualified-opportunity rate, and it inherits the customer-facing control set.

Deliberately absent: autonomous outbound. Disclosure duties apply, recipients are strangers, failures are public, and the reputational downside is not priced into any vendor deck.

## The plan, step by step

1. **Define the trust progression for CRM writes**: structured fields first, narrative later, never both on day one. Done when write scopes are set in the CRM, not in a prompt.
2. **Ship account research to one team.** Done when reps open the brief unprompted and administrative hours per rep fall in measurement, not in survey.
3. **Ship hygiene proposals.** Rep approves each change from a queue. Done when field completeness rises and correction rate stays flat.
4. **Ship inbound qualification with disclosure on** and a mandate that stops at booking meetings. Done when response time drops and qualified-opportunity rate holds against the human baseline.
5. **Add forecast explanation.** The agent explains movement and flags inconsistencies; the manager forecasts. Done when the meeting shortens and the manager still owns the number.
6. **Keep pricing, discounts, and terms gated forever.** Commitments are human.

## Worked example: the inbound lead at 22:40

A prospect fills the form at 22:40. The agent responds in ninety seconds, discloses itself, qualifies against your criteria in four exchanges, disqualifies the freebie hunter politely, and books the qualified one into the right rep's Tuesday with the conversation attached. The rep walks in with context, not a cold name. Response time went from fourteen hours to two minutes, and the only thing the agent ever committed to was a calendar slot, because meetings are inside its mandate and prices are not.

## Your agent roster

| Agent | What it does | Autonomy |
|---|---|---|
| Account research agent | Assembles account context with provenance | Read-only |
| CRM hygiene agent | Proposes structured updates from interactions | Proposes; rep approves |
| Inbound qualification agent | Engages, qualifies, routes, books | Executes to booking; no commercial terms |
| Forecast analysis agent | Explains pipeline movement, flags inconsistency | Explains; manager forecasts |

## What stays human

Relationships, negotiation, pricing, promises, and the forecast number itself. The agent makes the rep informed; it does not make the rep.

## The economics

Administrative hours per rep per week, measured not surveyed; CRM field completeness and correction rate; inbound response time and qualified-opportunity rate. Revenue attribution is deliberately not on the list: sales results move with process changes shipped alongside agents, and this page will not help anyone claim a win rate they cannot isolate.

## Watchouts

- Sales and marketing absorb outsized budgets while ranking mid-pack for production agents; the concentration is real even where the study naming it is contested. Buy against measured hours, not hope.
- Product-usage and intent data is usually worse than the CRM; the research agent is only as good as the signals you actually have.
- The qualification agent is customer-facing: disclosure, escalation, retention, all of it, from day one.
