## Monday, 9:30, the people team

The query queue that used to eat the morning is a report now: several hundred policy, leave, and payroll questions answered overnight from a corpus the policy lead owns, in plain language, with the source policy linked on every answer. The onboarding agent has this week's five starters fully staged: accounts requested through identity rules, equipment ordered, first-week schedules drafted, and each hiring manager nudged about the one thing only they can do. The business partners spend the day on the work that needs a human: a difficult case, a team in trouble, a manager who needs coaching. No screening score decided anything about anyone. All of it deployable now.

Here is the whole plan.

## Start with these two use cases

1. **Employee queries.** Policy, benefits, leave, payroll, answered from a curated corpus with the source shown. First because volume is high, failures are contained, and satisfaction is immediate.
2. **Onboarding orchestration.** Coordinate provisioning, scheduling, and paperwork across systems, with access decided by identity rules. Second because it is pure coordination drudgery with a measurable first-week payoff.

Deliberately absent: agentic screening or ranking of candidates, and any autonomy in performance or disciplinary decisions. Employment decisions are high-risk under the EU AI Act, works-council duties attach in much of Europe, and merely formal human review does not count as oversight.

## The plan, step by step

1. **Appoint the policy-corpus owner** with a review cadence tied to policy changes. Done when every answer cites a current policy.
2. **Ship queries on the top intents.** Escalation to a business partner wired with context. Done when resolution holds and reopens fall.
3. **Consult early where consultation applies.** The evidence says consultation does not slow adoption; contested adoption is what fails. Done when the workforce representatives have seen the design before launch, not after.
4. **Ship onboarding orchestration.** Entitlements decided by identity rules; the agent coordinates, never grants. Done when day-one readiness is measured and rising.
5. **Adopt the portable clause** even where no union asks: no discipline based solely on a system's output. Done when it is written into policy.
6. **Keep recruitment assistance at summarize-and-evidence only,** if you use it at all, with reviewer override rates measured and materially above zero.

## Worked example: the parental-leave question

An employee asks a leave question that spans two policies and a recent legal change. The agent answers in plain language, cites both policies, flags the recent change with its effective date, and offers the business-partner escalation. The employee takes the answer; the interaction is logged with sources. The policy lead sees the question cluster in the weekly report, realises the policy wording is confusing people, and fixes the source document, which instantly improves every future answer. That loop, questions revealing unclear policy, is the quiet compounding value of this playbook.

## Your agent roster

| Agent | What it does | Autonomy |
|---|---|---|
| Query agent | Answers policy and service questions with sources | Resolves; escalates |
| Onboarding agent | Coordinates provisioning, scheduling, paperwork | Executes coordination; identity rules decide access |
| Workforce insight agent | Aggregate analysis for planning | Aggregate only; no individual inference |
| Screening assistant (optional) | Summarizes applications against stated criteria | Suggests only; reviewer decides |

## What stays human

Every employment consequence: hiring, performance, discipline, dismissal. Empathy in hard moments. And oversight that is real: show reviewers evidence, not scores, so a different answer is genuinely reachable.

## The economics

Query resolution and reopen rate, business-partner hours recovered for judgment work, day-one readiness for starters, and, if screening assistance exists, reviewer override rate as a rubber-stamping alarm. The recruitment agents are never costed on efficiency; an efficiency case for screening is a sign the classification work has not been done.

## Watchouts

- Employment is Annex III high-risk with obligations from 2 December 2027; the deferral moves the deadline, not the work.
- Worker monitoring triggers co-determination rights in several jurisdictions on capability, not intent; supervision telemetry is itself worker data.
- The solitary-versus-coordinated evidence applies inside HR too: query handling improves fast; performance cycles do not change until the humans agree new norms.
