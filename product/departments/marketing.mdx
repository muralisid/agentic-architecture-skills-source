## Wednesday, 10:00, the content room

The campaign that launches Friday was mechanically assembled overnight: variants built against the brief and the claims corpus, UTMs and audiences set, platform specs checked, everything queued for human eyes. The review queue is moving because generation was throttled to review capacity, a deliberate control, not a limitation. The performance agent explains yesterday's numbers instead of listing them: which creative moved, which audience saturated, with the sources shown. Localisation drafts for three markets carry each market's labelling rules already applied. Every synthetic asset is labelled because the pipeline does it, not because someone remembered. Nothing reached an audience unreviewed. All of it deployable now.

Here is the whole plan.

## Start with these two use cases

1. **Campaign operations.** Assembly, scheduling, and QA of campaign mechanics across platforms. First because it is bounded, verifiable, mechanical work where errors are visible before spend.
2. **Content drafting against a claims corpus.** Drafts grounded in brand truth and approved claims, with human publication approval. Second, and only after the claims corpus has an owner, because marketing agents generate claims, and unverifiable claims are a regulatory exposure with or without AI law.

Deliberately absent: autonomous publication of anything, anywhere.

## The plan, step by step

1. **Build the claims corpus and appoint its owner.** Every claim approved, dated, sourced. Done when drafting agents can only cite from it.
2. **Wire synthetic-content labelling into the pipeline.** It is law in more than one jurisdiction already. Done when labelling coverage is 100% and alarmed.
3. **Ship campaign operations.** Done when mechanical errors per launch fall and launch lead time shortens.
4. **Ship drafting throttled to review capacity.** Cheapest mistake in this department is producing more content than anyone can govern. Done when review queue age is stable while output rises.
5. **Add localisation with per-market rules.** Done when market compliance checks pass on the draft, not on the recall.
6. **Add performance explanation.** The agent explains; the marketer decides where the budget goes.

## Worked example: the Friday launch

Twelve variants, four audiences, three platforms. The operations agent assembles all thirty-six combinations, checks specs, applies naming and tracking conventions, labels the synthetic assets, and queues the set with a diff against the brief. The reviewer rejects two variants for tone; the rejection reasons are captured and tighten the next generation. Nothing shipped until a human said so, and the review took forty minutes because the queue arrived organised. The alternative Friday, hand-assembling thirty-six combinations, is the one your team remembers too well.

## Your agent roster

| Agent | What it does | Autonomy |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign operations agent | Assembles, schedules, QAs mechanics | Executes mechanics; launch gated |
| Drafting agent | Produces variants against brief and claims corpus | Drafts; human approves publication |
| Localisation agent | Adapts approved content per market rules | Drafts; market owner approves |
| Performance agent | Explains results with provenance | Explains only |

## What stays human

Creative direction, claims approval, cultural judgment, publication, and the budget. Review capacity is the constraint that grows; plan it like a production line, because it is one.

## The economics

Published assets per reviewer per week (the real capacity number), review rejection rate, labelling coverage at 100%, claims-corpus freshness, and output diversity measured rather than assumed. Cost per asset falling while review capacity is unchanged is a queue, not a saving.

## Watchouts

- Slide and asset generation was faster and markedly worse in observed-task testing; speed is not the metric on creative output.
- The homogenisation question is unresolved in the research; measure your output diversity rather than assuming it.
- Labelling is a pipeline capability, not a policy statement; China's rules are in force and the EU's transparency duties are live.
