Every agentic enterprise, whatever its industry, is the same seven planes. Your estate plugs into them; your industry page shows them wired to your systems. This is the generic picture for everyone else.

<GuideFigure id="seven-plane-architecture" fullWidth />

**Execution** is where agent work runs. **Action** is how agents reach your systems: tool servers wrapping the APIs you already govern, carrying the caller's identity. **Knowledge** is what agents know: curated, per-purpose corpora with named owners, never one giant index. **Control** is what is allowed: registry, identity, policy decisions, budgets, kill switches. **Improvement** is how behaviour changes: evaluations gate every change, and rollback exists. **Evidence** is what you can prove afterward, collected outside the agent's reach. **Human** is who decides, supervises, and answers for it all.

Two rules hold the picture together, and they are the two most often broken:

1. **Enforcement lives in the control plane, never in the execution plane.** An instruction in a prompt is a preference. The same rule enforced at a gateway is a control.
2. **The evidence plane is fed by collection the agent cannot influence.** Anything an agent reports about itself is testimony, not evidence.

<GuideFigure id="three-target-architectures" fullWidth />

The same planes, three builds: a regulated enterprise runs two estates and doubles nothing else; a mid-market firm rents the control plane and builds only the knowledge plane; a digital native has the capability and must add the discipline. Your industry and department pages apply the right build for you.
