Four zones, one rule: **models may inform, never decide.**

<GuideFigure id="four-deterministic-zones" fullWidth />

1. **Access and entitlements.** Who may touch what is decided by rules over verified identity. Risk models feed the rule; they never are the rule.
2. **Movement of money.** Payments execute against scoped, revocable mandates. This is not caution; it is how card networks already work, and all three major agentic payment protocols, built independently, landed on the same shape.
3. **Safety actuation.** Machine learning is excluded from safety functions by the standards themselves. Agents advise operators; they do not touch the protection layer.
4. **Formal regulatory records.** Drafting with a model is fine. Attestation is a human act with a name attached, because regulators require accountability, not typing.

Everything outside these zones is negotiable and should be negotiated ambitiously. Inside them, the boundary is what lets your regulator, your auditor, and your board say yes to everything else.

One honest caveat: the deterministic gate bounds the damage; it does not protect what the agent wants. One payment protocol was successfully red-teamed with prompt injection operating entirely within its mandate bounds. Injection defense and human confirmation of intent stay mandatory even with perfect gates.
