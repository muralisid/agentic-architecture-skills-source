Your industry and department pages already name your starters. When you rank your own candidates beyond those, run each through three gates. A use case that fails any gate is not ranked lower; it is not ranked.

<GuideFigure id="use-case-three-gate-funnel" fullWidth />

1. **Can a domain expert write twenty to fifty pass-or-fail checks for it, from real failures?** If nobody can say what correct looks like, nothing downstream can improve it. Valuable ideas that fail here go to the make-it-evaluable queue: fund the definition of success, not the agent, and it is the highest-return spend in a young programme.
2. **Does its value depend on a model deciding access, money, safety, or a formal record?** Then reshape it so the model informs and a rule decides, or drop it. This gate kills the demos that could never ship.
3. **Does a curated, owned corpus exist for it, or can one exist this quarter?** More documents is not readiness: accuracy collapsed in a documented case when a corpus grew twentyfold uncurated.

Rank the survivors by value per resolved outcome against cost per resolved outcome, discounted by how reversible a failure is. Then check one portfolio-level constraint everyone misses: **your approved set must fit inside your people's supervision capacity in bursts**, because workloads are approved one at a time and supervised all at once.

Write the kill criteria at funding time, not after the sunk cost. Stopping a use case that missed its numbers is the process working.
