Agents loop. A task is many model calls, tool calls, and retries; vendor-published figures put agentic work at roughly 4x plain chat for single agents and 15x for multi-agent systems. Someone absorbs that multiplier, and your pricing regime decides who:

- **Per-user seats**: the vendor absorbs the loop; you pay for people who may barely use it. The trap is paying per employee for one assistant action per day, which a real evaluation actually recorded.
- **Per-agent seats**: fits few durable agents; punishes fleets.
- **Metered APIs**: you absorb the loop; ephemeral fleets get cheap, and you discover the multiplier in production unless budgets are enforced in the platform.

<GuideFigure id="cost-per-resolved-outcome" fullWidth />

Whatever the meter says, **decide on cost per resolved outcome**:

Run cost divided by resolution rate, **plus supervision minutes at a loaded rate, plus the failure rate times the cost of a wrong outcome.** The second term is a real capacity constraint your business case probably omits, and the third dominates anywhere statements bind externally, because courts have held companies responsible for what their chatbots say.

Divide by resolution, never by containment: a conversation that ends without help is contained and worthless, and every documented AI-first reversal set a containment or headcount target. Track repeat contact beside resolution, because re-contact multiplies true cost while the dashboard improves.

Where tokens are the wrong unit entirely, count the unit your business manages: truck rolls, analyst minutes, planner hours, days of backlog.
