One ladder is not enough. **Autonomy** (A0 manual to A5 governed lights-out) says who executes. **Learning** (L0 fixed policy to L3 continuous inside guardrails) says whether the agent improves and under what control. They move independently, and every workload sits somewhere on the grid, deliberately.

<GuideFigure id="autonomy-learning-matrix" fullWidth />

Three rules connect the axes:

1. **High autonomy requires governed learning.** A4 and above needs L2 or better, because at that level reliability must be improvable, not just observable.
2. **High autonomy requires proven oversight capacity**, calculated with wait time included and expressed as a burst rate. A level you cannot supervise is a level you have not reached.
3. **Without a learning loop you have a fixed-policy agent, not a teammate.** Fixed policy is the right choice for many workloads; just do not call it what it is not.

Use the grid per workload, never per enterprise. Your service desk triage might honestly sit at A3/L2 while your control-room assistant sits at A2/L1 forever, and both are correct. Claiming a level the instrumentation cannot prove is the most common self-deception in this field; the grid is for placing work honestly, not for scoring ambition.
