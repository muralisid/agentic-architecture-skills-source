# How the guide is organised

Editorial choices for the September 2026 revision. These are the author's choices about the guide, not external evidence for a technical recommendation.

## Start with the reader's question

Business readers begin with a useful task and a measurable result. Architects begin with the enterprise layers and the system around an agent. Both paths explain how to distill model intelligence into an agentic enterprise.

## Keep six clear entrances

[Use cases](/use-cases), [Architecture](/architecture), [Intelligence ladder](/ladder), [Knowledge and memory](/memory), [Research](/research), and [Skills](/skills) provide different ways into shared material.

## Use practical industrial examples

Power, water, and mining examples anchor the explanations. Department designs and other industry examples remain available. Proposed extensions are distinguished from the author's past project experience.

## Explain with images and progressive detail

Begin with the overall idea, expand a component, and link to technical depth. Each illustration has a text explanation. Phone layouts use readable images and accessible controls.

## Keep evidence separate from proposals

External claims need dated external sources. Vendor reports are identified. Author experiments carry methods and limitations. Existing writing is material to question and improve. It is not proof that a claim is correct.

## Keep the useful depth

The fourteen layers, seven planes, two boundaries, four protected action areas, ten cross-cutting concerns, and twenty-five technology choices remain available. They are the guide's reference framework, not a claim of universal consensus.
